## 1 · Why the source is many files

The RUNNING organism is already one body in the only sense that executes: one process (one PID), one
shared memory, one moment-thread (creature/mind.py) with its sense-door threads around it
(creature/body.py), all reading and writing the same organism state. The many files are the body's
TISSUES at rest — the way a human's retina and cochlea are distinct tissues of one organism. Nothing
about the separation is computational: `genome/machinery.py` and `creature/mind.py` share one heap at
runtime.

The costs of literally concatenating ~25,000 lines into one file, priced honestly:

- `genome/machinery.py` is PINNED (cache.WORLD_CODE): its bytes anchor every bench cache, witness and
  replay identity since 09-19. A merge costs a fresh run root, every cache redrawn, array-by-array
  identity checks with planted bits — weeks of gate work, for zero behavioral gain.
- Every suite and every worktree imports module paths (`creature.senses.video`, `genome.predict`).
  One file breaks every reference at once; the whole test discipline would need rebuilding in one move.
- The law culture reads file:line. A PREREG's adversarial walk against one monolith stops being
  reviewable — and reviewability is how the last month's defects were caught before they ran.

## 2 · Why not "multi-dimensional parallelism" inside one file

Python runs one thread of bytecode at a time (the GIL). The organism's real parallelism is already where
parallelism pays on this substrate: numpy's vectorized math releases the GIL (the heavy inner products
run in C), and the sense doors/think thread are I/O-and-timing threads around the one mind thread. True
multi-core execution would mean multiple PROCESSES — separate memory, messages instead of shared state:
that is the OPPOSITE of one body. The single process with shared memory is the one-body form for Python.
