### a.2 Words, and what a word needs to be a word
- A word is cut from a stream by statistics, then given a meaning by cross-situational evidence (rows above). The meaning is evidence about the scene the word was HEARD in; Roy et al. 2015, PNAS (one child, an ultradense home recording): «measures of the distinctiveness of the spatial, temporal, and linguistic contexts in which a word appears … are stronger predictors of learning than frequency of use and … play a consistent role across different syntactic categories» [opened]. For the organism this is the line between a COUNT and a CONTEXT: the brain's per-word meeting counts (census) are counts; a word's distinct contexts are not yet a recorded quantity.
- Mutual exclusivity (a child assumes a new word names a thing that has no name yet): Markman & Wachtel 1988 [record opened, text not; [from memory] the standard result]. The organism has this as test 6, one meeting (CHILD-LADDER L18).

### a.3 From words to sentences: item-based constructions, not rules
- Tomasello 2000, Trends Cogn Sci: «the vast majority of young children's early language is organized around concrete, item-based linguistic schemas. From this beginning, children then construct more abstract and adult-like linguistic constructions, but only gradually and in piecemeal fashion … children imitatively learn concrete linguistic expressions from the language they hear around them, and then — using their general cognitive and social-cognitive skills — categorize, schematize and creatively combine these individually learned expressions and structures» [opened; the body read, F5].
- Lieven 2003 and Bannard 2009 (table): a child's novel utterance is, in most cases, a small change to what the CHILD HERSELF had said before (Lieven: one child, one hour; Bannard: two children, each tested on her or his own later speech); productivity is limited at 2 and rises at 3. **[final pass F3, F4] These studies describe what a child CAN SAY; neither shows how a schema is LEARNED** (Bannard: «we have not addressed how the child acquires the categorical knowledge»; Tomasello 2000 [body] lists «on what basis do children make analogies or form schemas?» as outstanding). L1's step 2 is therefore a DESIGN HYPOTHESIS, not a human mechanism. What the bodies do add: nouns generalise first (Bannard: +6 / +13 coverage points at 2;0 from a noun category, +2 / +3 from verbs; Tomasello: novel nouns used freely at 22 months, novel verbs rarely before 3 and then gradually through the third and fourth years) and the early inventory is small (Tomasello: 92 % of 12 children's first multiword utterances came from one of their first 25 lexically based patterns; Bannard: 802–1,898 signs at 2;0, about 5,400 at 3;0).
- **Frames with slots are how word categories are found.** Mintz 2003, Cognition: «A frame is defined as two jointly occurring words with one word intervening … words that were surrounded by the same frequent frame were categorized together. The results show that frequent frames yield very accurate categories» [opened]. This is the organism's «frame with a slot» in his notes (p.5), and it is a FRAME OF TWO WORDS WITH A GAP, not the cells' co-occurring PAIR (`genome/sentence.py:30`): the gap is what makes the filler class visible.
- **Variation makes the frame visible.** Gómez 2002 (table). Onnis, Waterfall & Edelman 2008, Cognition: «Variation set structure — partial overlap of successive utterances in child-directed speech — has been shown to correlate with progress in children's acquisition of syntax. We demonstrate the benefits of variation set structure directly: … arranging a certain proportion of utterances in a training corpus in variation sets facilitated word and phrase constituent learning in adults» [opened].
- **Learning is learning to PROCESS.** Christiansen & Chater 2016, BBS: «the brain must compress and recode linguistic input as rapidly as possible … language acquisition is learning to process, rather than inducing, a grammar». McCauley & Christiansen 2019, Psychol Rev: a model that «learns in a purely incremental fashion, through online processing based on chunking … When the model encounters an utterance made by the target child, it attempts to generate an identical utterance using the same chunks and statistics involved during comprehension» — evaluated «across 79 single-child corpora spanning English, French, and German» (comprehension) and «over 200 single-child corpora representing 29 languages» (production) [opened both]. **Production reuses comprehension's own chunks [final pass F1]** — in the paper's sense: given the BAG of the words of a real child utterance, CBL orders its chunks by chunk-to-chunk backward transitional probability, greedily (58.5 % exact reproductions over 43 English corpora against a trigram baseline's 45.0 %; 20 adults ordered the same chunks at 95.6 % for the grammatical utterances and 64 % for the ungrammatical ones). The authors state that it «cannot produce utterances from scratch» (the bag always comes from a real utterance), has no meaning side and no abstraction, that class-based statistics did WORSE than item-based ones, and that it keeps local transitions, not whole utterances. It is evidence for ORDERING known content by chunk statistics and for item-based first; it is not evidence for choosing what to say.
- **Grammar by compression, with no neural network.** Stolcke & Omohundro 1994 (arXiv cmp-lg/9409010, abstract): «samples are incorporated by adding ad-hoc rules to a working grammar; subsequently, elements of the model (such as states or nonterminals) are merged to achieve generalization and a more compact representation. The choice of what to merge and when to stop is governed by the Bayesian posterior probability of the grammar given the data, which formalizes a trade-off between a close fit to the data and a default preference for simpler models ('Occam's Razor')». Solan, Horn, Ruppin & Edelman 2005, PNAS (ADIOS): «our unsupervised algorithm recursively distills from it hierarchically structured patterns … capable of learning complex syntax, generating grammatical novel sentences». Kolodny, Lotem & Edelman 2015, Cogn Sci: «incrementally learns a grammar that captures its statistical patterns, which can then be used to parse or generate new data. The grammar … takes the form of a directed weighted graph, whose nodes are recursively (hierarchically) defined patterns over the elements of the input stream» [opened all three]. These are the pure-road relatives of the Toy C compressor (§(d) L1). **[final pass F6, body of Stolcke & Omohundro]** Their criterion is the posterior on the TRAINING sample (a description-length prior plus Dirichlet parameter priors), not a held-out gain; their «preliminary experiments» on realistic corpora «yielded mixed results»; the successes are formal languages and toy grammars (100 random samples of a relative-clause grammar); a greedy merge works for HMM states and class merging but not for the SCFG chunking step (beam search, widths 3–10); and more data blocks a generalisation. The models are generative distributions (HMM, class-based n-gram, SCFG): only their model-selection criterion is borrowed (L1 step 4), as a MEASURE.

### a.4 Producing a sentence: a pipeline with a monitor
- Levelt, Roelofs & Meyer 1999, BBS: «We generate them two or three per second in fluent conversation … After a first stage of conceptual preparation, word generation proceeds through lexical selection, morphological and phonological encoding, phonetic encoding, and articulation itself. In addition, the speaker exerts some degree of output control, by monitoring of self-produced internal and overt speech» [opened].
- The look leads the mouth. Griffin & Bock 2000, Psychol Sci: «response-relevant information was rapidly extracted from scenes, allowing speakers to select grammatical subjects based on comprehended events rather than salience. When speaking extemporaneously, speakers began fixating pictured elements less than a second before naming them within their descriptions, a finding consistent with incremental lexical encoding. Eye movements anticipated the order of mention despite changes in picture orientation, in who-did-what-to-whom, and in sentence structure» [opened]. ### a.5 Monitoring one's own speech — two mechanisms, both documented
- **Through comprehension (the perceptual loop).** Postma 2000, Cognition (a review of three theories): «The perception-based approach proposes that the same mechanism employed in understanding other-produced language, the speech comprehension system, is also used to monitor one's own speech production … From the empirical evidence presently available, it is argued for a central perception-based monitor, potentially augmented with a few automatic, production-based error detection devices» [opened; abstract only — the body is paywalled and is known here only through Nozari et al.'s account, [second-hand], F7].
- **Through conflict inside production.** Nozari, Dell & Schwartz 2011, Cogn Psychol: «error detection is accomplished by monitoring response conflict via a frontal brain structure, such as the anterior cingulate cortex … Our results show a strong correlation between patients' error-detection ability and the model's characterization of their production skills, and no significant correlation between error detection and comprehension measures, thus supporting a production-based monitor» (aphasic patients) [opened]. The organism already holds a number of this kind: the MARGIN between the best and the second candidate (b, `produce_believed`). **[final pass F7, body]** The paper's conflict is exactly that kind of number — the difference between the two most active candidates at the moment of SELECTION, in the form −ln diff(max) (the standard deviation over all candidates did the same, more noisily) — and its results are quantitative: 29 aphasic patients, 384 semantic and 461 phonological errors of which 65 % and 57 % were detected; detection tracked the production weights of the SAME layer (r = .59, p = .001 semantic; r = .43, p = .021 phonological; the other layer's weights −.34 and −.55) and no comprehension test (every |r| ≤ .24; the mean of eight −.01). In the simulations the detection index (Cohen's d) fell from 3.26 to 1.41 to 0.37 as a production weight was degraded. Healthy speakers detect about HALF of their errors (≈ 50 %, from speech corpora, as the paper cites), and the monitors target SLIPS (hasty selection errors), not mistakes of knowledge. The perceptual loop is not dismissed: the paper questions its role as the PRIMARY self-monitor; the two assumptions in doubt are that speakers routinely monitor inner speech WHILE speaking aloud (an eye-movement study found no sign of it) and that comprehension is the basis of self-error detection (patients with good comprehension and poor detection, and the reverse); hybrid accounts exist (the paper cites Postma 2000 among them).
- **What this licenses:** the organism may use BOTH (a read-back through its own parse, and a conflict margin), and the two are separable experimentally (a plant of known errors; (g)). It may not claim either is «the» human mechanism: the literature disagrees (Postma 2000 vs Nozari 2011). **[final pass F7]** The human evidence ranks them: conflict at selection is the better supported PRIMARY monitor, taken before the sentence is said; the read-back is the design's second check, and it meets MacKay's objection, which the paper quotes against any monitor that compares the output with the correct one: «if an editor knows the correct output all along, one wonders why the correct output wasn't generated in the first place». In the organism the mouth's believed names and its read-back are two directions of ONE learner (`produce_believed`: the name is the delta rule's argmax for the thing, the read-back is the same learner's pointing from the word back to the thing), so the read-back catches a phrase that does not point back at its thing, not a wrong association that both directions share.

### a.6 Inner speech
- Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015, Psychol Bull: «Inner speech — also known as covert speech or verbal thinking — has been implicated in theories of cognitive development, speech monitoring, executive function, and psychopathology … inner speech appears to perform significant functions in human cognition, which in some cases reflect its developmental origins and its sharing of resources with other cognitive processes» [opened]. **[final pass F10, body, about 45 % read]** Inner speech is not one thing: the review separates a «minimal» account (covert articulation — the Watsonian view that most laboratory studies assume) from a multicomponent one (condensed against expanded, abstract against concrete, inner speaking against inner hearing), and Vygotsky took «speech minus sound» to be mistaken as the whole story; private speech arises about 2–3, is common 3–8 and peaks about 5; the strategic use of inner speech in memory is dated about 7 (contested by Jarrold & Citroen); the phonological loop (a passive store of 1–2 s plus active rehearsal, «inner speech or something very similar») may exist from about 18 months and serve language learning; inner speech serves self-regulation and executive function as a domain-general tool, its monitoring role being «a common view in psycholinguistic research» that the review defers to others; and language as the MEDIUM of thought «does not necessarily follow».
- It is production without the last stages. Oppenheim & Dell 2008, Cognition: «While lexical bias was present in both inner and overt speech errors, the phonemic similarity effect was evident only for overt errors … inner speech is impoverished at lower (featural) levels, but robust at higher (phonemic) levels» [opened; body]. **[final pass F9, body — the sentence «Words and their order survive the loss of articulation; the sound does not» of drafts 1–3 is WITHDRAWN]:** the paper tests lexical bias and phonemic similarity in the self-reported slips of 48 adults reciting tongue-twisters, and says nothing about word order. Its results: 1,217 of 6,144 recitations carried an error; lexical bias in both overt (48 against 21 slips) and inner speech (36 against 20); the similarity effect only overt (66 against 36; inner 39 against 52; interaction p = .023). The authors' own limits: the impoverishment may lie in production OR in perception and the experiment cannot tell which; it «may not hold true» for the inner speech that monitors overt speech, which may be «more fully specified». The review adds that Corley et al. 2011 found phoneme substitutions in inner speech, that Oppenheim & Dell's 2010 follow-up found them with silent mouthing (their «flexible abstraction»), and that self-report is a limit. The paper's introduction (the standard view, not its finding): inner speech is the basis of rehearsal in short-term memory.
- Its sensory content comes from a prediction. Scott, Yeung, Gick & Werker 2013, J Acoust Soc Am: «the auditory content of inner speech is provided by corollary discharge. Corollary discharge is the motor system's prediction of the sensory consequences of its actions. This prediction can bias perception of other sensations» [opened]. (So hearing one's own voice is not new evidence about the world: a design fact for the ear, (d) L4.) **[final pass F11, second-hand]** The review (body) calls the forward-model account one of several: where inner speech sits in it «is not always clear» («no external percept or motor consequence to be attenuated if no sound is created»), such self-monitoring accounts «have been criticized» on the psychopathology evidence, and the failure the account is used for in that literature is the misattribution of inner speech to an external source (auditory verbal hallucinations). The L4 self-voice rule is therefore NOT derived from the human account: it rests on the pure-road law (a closed loop is refused), and the human account is an analogy for it.
- It develops from talk to others, to talk to oneself, to thought: Winsler 2003 (table) [opened]; Vygotsky's account [body, second-hand: the review's section on his theory].

### a.7 Talking about oneself
- **A self-concept in stages.** Rochat 2003, Conscious Cogn: «5 levels of self-awareness are presented and discussed as they chronologically unfold from the moment of birth to approximately 4–5 years of age» [opened]. CDC 30 months: «Says words like "I," "me," or "we"»; 3 years: «Says first name, when asked» (table).
- **Self-reference has two forms and the CDC does not order them.** It lists «I, me, we» at 30 months and «Says first name, when asked» at 3 years — two different skills (a pronoun used, a question answered with one's name), so the pages establish no order between referring to oneself by name and by pronoun. The pronoun-reversal error («you» said for oneself, learned from speech addressed to the child) is Charney 1980 and Oshima-Takane 1988 [records opened, text not; [from memory]: children who hear speech between OTHER people, not addressed to them, learn I/you correctly; those who only hear addressed speech reverse them; and toddlers often name themselves before they say «I»].
- **Talk about the mind is taught by talk about the mind.** Meins et al. 2002, Child Dev: «mothers' use of appropriate mental state comments independently predicted overall ToM performance, accounting for 11% of the variance» (comments at 6 months, tasks at 45 and 48 months). Ruffman, Slade & Crowe 2002, Child Dev: «Mothers' use of mental state utterances in these descriptions at early time points was consistently correlated with later theory-of-mind understanding … Mothers' mental state utterances seemed genuinely causal because early theory-of-mind ability was not related to later mother mental state utterances … Results also showed that children's desire talk preceded their talk about beliefs» [opened both]. (Mental verbs, Shatz et al. 1983, [record opened, text not; [from memory] «want» before «think» and «know», across the third year].)
- **Uncertainty is reported early, nonverbally first** (Goupil 2016, table): at 20 months a child that has been TAUGHT the option asks the caregiver for help selectively. **[final pass F8, body]** The paper's own introduction says children under 4 «often fail to provide accurate metacognitive judgments» in words and overestimate their knowledge, and that talk about mental states begins in the third year (its references, not opened); «says "I don't know" in action months before it says it in words» of drafts 1–3 was an inference, and what the body shows is the nonverbal ask. The earliest verbal epistemic form in Tomasello's Box 3 [body] is a FIXED HEDGE: «I think» used for the child's own uncertainty, meaning roughly «maybe», never negated and never in the past.
- **A life story is built with an adult's help.** Nelson & Fivush 2004, Psychol Rev: autobiographical memory «emerges gradually across the preschool years. The components that contribute … include basic memory abilities, language and narrative, adult memory talk, temporal understanding, and understanding of self and others» [opened].

### a.8 Conversation and contingency
- Turn-taking: Stivers et al. 2009, PNAS: «a general avoidance of overlapping talk and a minimization of silence between conversational turns … within a range of 250 ms from the cross-language mean» [opened]. CDC 3 years: two back-and-forth exchanges.
- **Contingent speech teaches; prerecorded speech does not.** Goldstein & Schwade 2008, Psychol Sci (9.5-month-olds): «Infants given contingent feedback rapidly restructured their babbling, incorporating phonological patterns from caregivers' speech, but infants given noncontingent feedback did not». Kuhl, Tsao & Liu 2003, PNAS: «exposure to recorded Mandarin, without interpersonal interaction, had no effect … infants show phonetic learning from live, but not prerecorded, exposure to a foreign language». Hirsh-Pasek et al. 2015, Psychol Sci: the quality of early parent–child communication at 24 months «accounted for 27% of the variance in expressive language 1 year later … considerably more potent predictors … than was the quantity of mothers' words» [opened all]. **[final pass F12, abstract-only]** The first two concern PHONOLOGICAL patterns of babbling and phonetic learning from live against recorded speech; extending them to «a narrator's contingent TRUE SENTENCES teach self-reports» is an ANALOGY (and Meins 2002 and Ruffman 2002 above are correlational); (d) L6 R4 and T-L4 carry it as a hypothesis, with the RECORDED and SHUFFLED arms as its test.

### a.9 What the human data say about the DESIGN (the seven lines (d) is built on)
1. **Comprehension first.** Words are understood at 6 months, said at about 12; a mouth is built after, and out of, the ear's road (Pickering & Garrod; McCauley & Christiansen).
2. **Early sentences are schemas with a slot**, almost never a free combination; productivity is limited at 2 and grows at 3 (Lieven; Bannard). **A grammar of rules is not the first thing to build.** [final pass F3–F5: the evidence is for WHAT early speech is (item-based, nouns generalising first; McCauley & Christiansen: class-based statistics did worse), not for HOW schemas are learned.]
3. **A frame is found where its middle varies** (Gómez; Mintz; Onnis): the learner needs variation sets, i.e. the same sentence with one element changed, close together in the speech it hears.
4. **A message precedes the words** (Levelt: conceptual preparation first), and **the eye leads the mouth** (Griffin & Bock).
5. **Two monitors** (Postma; Nozari): a read-back through comprehension and a conflict signal inside production. [final pass F7: the conflict signal is the better supported primary; healthy speakers catch about half their slips.]
6. **Inner speech is the same pipeline minus articulation**, its content a prediction that is not mistaken for the world (Oppenheim & Dell; Scott). [final pass F9–F11: one account of several (the minimal, Watsonian one), and the prediction story is contested.]
7. **Self-talk is learned from talk about oneself that is true and contingent** (Meins; Ruffman; Goldstein & Schwade; Kuhl); **uncertainty is reported before it is said** (Goupil); **the pronoun is the hard part of self-reference** (Charney, Oshima-Takane [from memory]), which is why the design below starts from a name (a design choice, not a claim about the child's order). [final pass F8, F12: the contingent-speech evidence is phonological and correlational, an analogy; the child's nonverbal ask was taught in the experiment; verbal calibration is poor under 4.]

## (d) Mechanisms proposed

**The shape in one paragraph.** A sentence is built the way the human data say it is (a.9). A MESSAGE comes first (L2). A CONSTRUCTION the creature has heard and kept carries it (L1, L3). The brain's existing phrase mouth fills the noun-phrase slot (L3 calls `produce_believed` unchanged). Nothing here needs a new kind of learner: the learners are the ones already in the body — a delta rule over words and things, and (Toy C) a compressor that keeps what shortens held-out text. The only new object is a **store of constructions**. What no sentence can do without is in L6: **a world that speaks sentences** (gap 2).

**The pure-road audit — the compile's refusal test, written so it can be run as a test and not argued.** For every mechanism: (i) what is LEARNED; (ii) from WHAT — only heard utterances and the organism's own signals; (iii) no weight, table, lexicon or embedding comes from outside the life; (iv) no step defines, scores or samples a distribution over the vocabulary conditioned on the words already said. (iv) is a test in (e): **SLOT-INDEPENDENCE, with a planted offender.** Named and refused here: a small language model as a «surface realiser» or «sentence smoother», a pretrained tokenizer or sentence embedding, a model that is trained to predict the next word given a message (the human-faithful connectionist rival is exactly that, [from memory] Chang, Dell & Bock 2006 — not opened, not adopted). **[final pass F1, F6] Two near-misses found in the bodies, named so that nobody imports them.** (1) McCauley & Christiansen's production step orders a message's own chunks by chunk-to-chunk backward transitional probability, greedily. It is not a distribution over the vocabulary — the bag IS the message — so (iv) as written would not forbid it, but SLOT-INDEPENDENCE does (the next chunk depends on the previous one): refused as a component, kept as a comparator arm (T-L1). (iv) is therefore restated for the MOUTH: no step ranks or samples the next token or chunk of an utterance conditioned on what has already been said. (2) Stolcke & Omohundro's models are generative distributions and their class-based n-gram is a next-word model: only their model-selection criterion is borrowed, as a MEASURE of heard text (L1 step 4); no HMM, class n-gram or SCFG they induce is ever sampled or argmaxed for the mouth.

### L1 · SCHEMA — the store of constructions and the compressor that keeps them (extends Toy C)
- **What it is.** A construction is a sequence of tokens, each a CONSTANT (a heard word) or a SLOT of a kind (one of the brain's learned types, or a distributional class), plus a MEANING record (L6), a support count, and `gain`: the bits it saves on held-out heard utterances, net of its own description (Toy C §1: `G(F) = bits(held-out | ∅) − bits(held-out | F) − L(F)`, with a slotted construction coded as one token plus its filler under the slot's own counts).
- **How it is learned — each step is a human mechanism of (a):**
  1. REMEMBER heard utterances whole, as schemas with no slot (Tomasello 2000: concrete expressions first). [final pass F5, body: confirmed — «children imitatively learn concrete linguistic expressions from the language they hear around them». CBL differs: it keeps no whole utterances, only local transitions (F1); L1 keeps a rolling store for its sleep-time induction.]
  2. ABSTRACT by ONE-OPERATION SUBSTITUTION (Lieven 2003): two stored utterances that differ in exactly one position, the two words being of one kind, become one schema with a slot of that kind. Where the kind is unknown the slot opens a candidate class, found by the FRAME the words share (Mintz 2003: the words that stand between the same two constants); VARIATION SETS (Onnis 2008, L6 R2) make it visible sooner. **[final pass F3–F5: a DESIGN HYPOTHESIS.** Lieven's analysis traces a child's novel utterances to her OWN earlier ones (one child, one hour) and shows what she could say, not how a schema is learned; Bannard 2009 says outright that it did not address how categories are acquired; Tomasello 2000 lists «on what basis do children make analogies or form schemas?» as outstanding and suggests a critical mass of exemplars (the number of DIFFERENT fillers heard in a construction). Two utterances make a CANDIDATE here; the held-out gain of step 3 is what supplies the critical mass. Nouns first: the human data put the noun category earliest (Bannard; Tomasello), which matches an organism whose slot kinds are the brain's learned word types.]
  3. KEEP by HELD-OUT COMPRESSION: a schema stays only while `gain > 0` on the held-out 40 % of the recent utterances; otherwise pruned. This is his own notes' rule (RESEARCH-2026-10-03 §0 row 2: «frames with slots … pruned when they stop paying off») and the lifelong grow-and-prune cycle of the map (B10). **[final pass F3, F6]** No human study gives this rule: Bannard 2009 uses held-out coverage and perplexity as the TEST of a grammar and induces by an MCMC search under a prior for fewer signs; Stolcke & Omohundro's criterion is a posterior on the TRAINING sample. A held-out gain with a description cost is Toy C's criterion and his notes', and that is its whole standing until Toy C is read. Scale, for the store: the induced grammars held 802 to 1,898 signs at 2;0 and about 5,400 at 3;0 (3–6 categories).
  4. MERGE CLASSES by a description-length criterion in the manner of Stolcke & Omohundro 1994: two slot classes merge when the merged model's description length is lower. An optional step for later rungs. **[final pass F6]** In the paper this is the cheap case — greedy merging is reported to work for HMM states and class merging and to fail for chunking in SCFGs (beam search, widths 3–10) — their posterior is computed on the training sample, and more data makes a merge LESS likely (100 times the samples, 100 times the likelihood loss of a generalisation): a property T-L1 reads (the oracle grammar's classes must merge at the oracle's own counts). Only the criterion is borrowed, as a measure.
- **When.** At SLEEP, the body's own consolidation (`SLEEP every 2500 moments`, [genome :68]; [STATUS] `sleeps 124`), never on the canvas path.
- **Interface.** READS the ear's utterances in order with their source and moment (`words_in_instant`, stored per episode, `genome/fast.py:81–88, 142`, and read the way `find_frames` reads it, `genome/sentence.py:55–62`), `Brain.types()` (brain.py:441–445), `Brain.census()` (only MATURE words may fill a slot: `mature` is the census flag, brain.py:274–275). It is a reader and a store, never a second writer of memory (his 09-17 invariants: one owner of memory; the replay gate).

### L2 · MESSAGE — the Intent record, its producers, the reasons to speak
- **What it is.** Before any word, the speaker holds an `Intent` {kind: REPORT | ANSWER | ASK | REFUSE; subject: SELF | WORLD; predicate: SEE | KNOW | NOT-KNOW | THINK | DO; args: a thing hypothesis, a word, an act record; reason: why now; evidence: the row it was read from}. Levelt: conceptual preparation precedes lexical selection; Griffin & Bock: the message is built from the comprehended event. It is journaled WITH the utterance, so the grader can compare what was said with what was meant.
- **Three producers, all READING records that exist** (this part defines no new knowledge of the self; that is R-SELF's):
  1. PERCEPT — the DRIVE turn's thing and its believed names (`Brain.turn`: `say`, `expected`, `held`, brain.py:505–561) → REPORT / SEE.
  2. EPISTEMIC — the same turn's doubt (`held` empty, the `none` flag, the margin) and, from the self, the per-word reliability (`SelfNode.reliability`, web.py:429) and the census's `mature` / `young` → KNOW / NOT-KNOW. **The interface to R-SELF is one call:** `self_view()` returns facts that are TRUE BY RECORD, each with a reference to the row that makes it true; what the self holds, and what its census counts, is R-SELF's design.
  3. THOUGHT — the INNER turn's row (`act: inner`: topic, topic_source, the remembered scene's say, surprise; mind.py:1796–1799) → THINK(topic), reason = topic_source.
- **The reasons to speak are a policy, replacing the DRIVE's `says 1` (every canvas):** speak when (a) ADDRESSED (L5); (b) the canvas is SURPRISING (`Brain.surprise`, brain.py:601–609, above a floor: Liszkowski 2004, pointing to share); (c) an epistemic state CHANGES (a claim is demoted; a word crosses `young` → `mature`: news to the listener); (d) a DOUBT is to be shared (the existing ask; Goupil 2016 — in the paper the ask is an option the infants were TAUGHT and then used selectively [final pass F8]). A TOLD-SET (what was said about which thing or state in the last W canvases) suppresses repeats: tell the one who does not know (Liszkowski 2008). The cells' mouth and the brain's say keep their own rows as the floor speakers.
- **Gene.** `SPEAK reasons addressed surprise change doubt; told window 20`.
- **Pure-road audit.** An Intent holds the organism's OWN signals (a percept, a flag, a journaled topic) and no words; its predicates are genome-level conceptual primitives, stated as priors like the self's claim quantifiers `always | usually` (web.py Claim), not learned from text. **A decision for the seat, not hidden:** the list SEE / KNOW / NOT-KNOW / THINK / DO is OURS.

### L3 · THE MOUTH BY CONSTRUCTION, with its two monitors
1. **SELECT** the constructions whose learned meaning unifies with the Intent (predicate, polarity, argument arity) and whose slots the Intent's arguments can fill with MATURE words. The most SPECIFIC wins (the elsewhere condition); ties by `gain`, then by name; an exact tie by a named substream, as `produce_believed` does (brain.py:549).
2. **FILL.** A thing-NP slot is filled BY `produce_believed`, unchanged: the brain's mouth becomes the noun-phrase formulator inside a sentence, and its read-back, margin and refusal reasons come with it. A word slot takes the Intent's word.
3. **LINEARISE** by the construction's own token order. There is no score over orderings of a vocabulary.
4. **MONITOR** — both a.5 mechanisms: **(i) CONFLICT**: the margin between the best and the runner-up construction and between the best and the runner-up filler (the number `produce_believed` already returns, learners.py:1232–1234); an Intent whose margin is under a floor is not said. **(ii) READ-BACK**: the produced sentence is PARSED by the same store that parses a heard one (the one-store-for-both-directions idea of Pickering & Garrod and of McCauley & Christiansen — [final pass F2] CBL itself has NO monitor: its quoted sentence is about the chunkatory's use in production; the read-back is this design's own); the recovered Intent is compared with the intended one slot by slot, the NP slot by `answer_point` (the existing check). An unequal read is a REPAIR — the next candidate, at most k = 3 — and then «I cannot say it yet», with a reason (the existing ones plus «no construction», «form», «slot»). **[final pass F7]** Human ranking and limits: conflict at selection (i) is the better supported PRIMARY monitor and is taken before the sentence is said, as the paper measures it; the read-back (ii) is a post-assembly, pre-say check, the analogue of the perceptual loop's INTERNAL channel, and the paper's objection to inner-channel monitoring (an echo of one's own voice while articulating) does not transfer, since a say here is a row with no concurrent articulation. Both monitors see SLIPS (a wrong candidate taken), not MISTAKES (knowledge lacking): the doubt and the census handle those (L2 EPISTEMIC). And the read-back shares its learner with the mouth, so it meets MacKay's objection («if an editor knows the correct output all along, one wonders why the correct output wasn't generated in the first place»): it catches a slot-selection slip or a phrase that does not point back, not a wrong association.
5. **SPEAK**: one `say` journal row with words, construction id, Intent, read-back and margin.
- **It must not break:** the cells' frame mouth (kept as the floor speaker BY NAME, as STAGE-2 part C says); the brain's scored answers (the NP road is untouched; a JOIN CHECK on the 2,209 / 2,222 / 2,221 answers, as every part since BRAIN-IN-BODY ran it).
- **Floors to beat:** `produce_believed` alone (the bare phrase) and `genome/chain.py` (the cells' chained frames). The new mouth must not lose truth against the bare phrase on the same canvases (a paired reading) and must be a sentence where they are not.
- **Pure-road audit.** SELECT is unification over finite sets; FILL is the brain's existing selection; LINEARISE is a stored order; MONITOR reads and compares. The SLOT-INDEPENDENCE test (e.0) is the proof of (iv). [final pass F1: LINEARISE is the construction's stored order, not a chunk-to-chunk transitional-probability ordering of a bag (McCauley & Christiansen's step), which would fail the test.]

### L4 · INNER SPEECH — the same pipeline without its last step, and the self-voice rule
1. **COVERT SAY.** L2 → L3 with articulation off: an `inner_say` row, no `say` row. The INNER turn's one-phrase says become construction sentences once L3 exists; the walk that picks the next thought (creature/inner.py:30–70) is unchanged. **[final pass F10]** This is the MINIMAL (Watsonian) account of inner speech — speech without articulation — and the review's body says Vygotsky held it mistaken as the whole story and that inner speech is multicomponent: a condensed form (note-form, the topic already known; the default setting in Fernyhough's model) and an expanded form (it keeps the phonology and the turn-taking of talk). The organism's existing INNER says are one phrase with no subject, which resemble the CONDENSED form; the construction sentence is the EXPANDED form. Expanded comes first here because the monitor needs the words in order (a design reason), not because anything in the review says a child's inner speech starts expanded.
2. **UTTERANCE BUFFER.** The last 3 heard and 3 own utterances (covert or overt) in a small ring inside the mind: the working-memory loop that the map's B9 says is absent («None. One item per think»). A two-turn exchange (L5) and rehearsal both need it. It holds WORDS: the organism's utterances are word-level, so Oppenheim & Dell's finding (lexical effects present in inner speech, the feature-level effect absent; contested, [final pass F9]) costs it nothing either way. The loop itself is supported by the review's body [F10]: a passive store of 1–2 s plus active rehearsal, which may exist from about 18 months and serve language learning.
3. **THE SELF-VOICE RULE.** An utterance the organism made, covert or overt, reaches its own learners only as a MONITOR input (the read-back), never as a training example. The ear tags any ear row equal to a recent own utterance `self`; the brain's learner and the schema learner skip it. This is the corollary-discharge analogue: a predicted consequence is not evidence about the world (Scott 2013) — an ANALOGUE only [final pass F11]: the human account is one of several and contested, and the rule rests on the pure-road law (a closed loop is refused), not on it. The human failure it guards against is the one the review's body calls misattribution of inner speech to an external source. And CBL is no precedent for the opposite: the utterance CBL re-processes is the child's REAL corpus utterance, not the model's output, and it made no significant difference (F2). Today it is harmless (`voice_rows 0`, its says are journal rows); it is written now because the pure road dies the day the creature's own sentences become its teacher.
4. **REHEARSAL, idle only** (the INNER condition: a body row with backlog 0): covert says of remembered scenes are scored by the monitor; a construction whose read-back keeps failing loses `gain` standing at the next sleep (the design's own rule [final pass F10]: the review's body supports inner speech as a domain-general tool for self-regulation, task maintenance and planning, and calls monitoring «a common view in psycholinguistic research» without grounding a demotion rule; the human date for strategic rehearsal is about 7 years, contested, so this is NOT a human rung).
- **Reads** the INNER row, the buffer, the monitor. **Writes** `inner_say` rows and the ring (inside the snapshot GENERATION the days law requires, mind.py `snapshot`).
- **Pure-road audit.** The self-voice rule IS the audit of this mechanism: without it, a closed loop; with it, an identity (e.1, L4 mark).

### e.7 What would show this proposal wrong (written before any build)
