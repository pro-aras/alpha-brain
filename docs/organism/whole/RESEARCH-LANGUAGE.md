### a.2 Words, and what a word needs to be a word
- A word is cut from a stream by statistics, then given a meaning by cross-situational evidence (rows above). The meaning is evidence about the scene the word was HEARD in; Roy et al. 2015, PNAS (one child, an ultradense home recording): «measures of the distinctiveness of the spatial, temporal, and linguistic contexts in which a word appears … are stronger predictors of learning than frequency of use and … play a consistent role across different syntactic categories» [opened]. For the organism this is the line between a COUNT and a CONTEXT: the brain's per-word meeting counts (census) are counts; a word's distinct contexts are not yet a recorded quantity.
- Mutual exclusivity (a child assumes a new word names a thing that has no name yet): Markman & Wachtel 1988 [record opened, text not; [from memory] the standard result]. The organism has this as test 6, one meeting (CHILD-LADDER L18).

### a.3 From words to sentences: item-based constructions, not rules
- Tomasello 2000, Trends Cogn Sci: «the vast majority of young children's early language is organized around concrete, item-based linguistic schemas. From this beginning, children then construct more abstract and adult-like linguistic constructions, but only gradually and in piecemeal fashion … children imitatively learn concrete linguistic expressions from the language they hear around them, and then — using their general cognitive and social-cognitive skills — categorize, schematize and creatively combine these individually learned expressions and structures» [opened].
- Lieven 2003 and Bannard 2009 (table): a child's novel utterance is a stored schema with one slot filled differently; productivity is limited at 2 and rises at 3.
- **Frames with slots are how word categories are found.** Mintz 2003, Cognition: «A frame is defined as two jointly occurring words with one word intervening … words that were surrounded by the same frequent frame were categorized together. The results show that frequent frames yield very accurate categories» [opened]. This is the organism's «frame with a slot» in his notes (p.5), and it is a FRAME OF TWO WORDS WITH A GAP, not the cells' co-occurring PAIR (`genome/sentence.py:30`): the gap is what makes the filler class visible.
- **Variation makes the frame visible.** Gómez 2002 (table). Onnis, Waterfall & Edelman 2008, Cognition: «Variation set structure — partial overlap of successive utterances in child-directed speech — has been shown to correlate with progress in children's acquisition of syntax. We demonstrate the benefits of variation set structure directly: … arranging a certain proportion of utterances in a training corpus in variation sets facilitated word and phrase constituent learning in adults» [opened].
- **Learning is learning to PROCESS.** Christiansen & Chater 2016, BBS: «the brain must compress and recode linguistic input as rapidly as possible … language acquisition is learning to process, rather than inducing, a grammar». McCauley & Christiansen 2019, Psychol Rev: a model that «learns in a purely incremental fashion, through online processing based on chunking … When the model encounters an utterance made by the target child, it attempts to generate an identical utterance using the same chunks and statistics involved during comprehension» — evaluated «across 79 single-child corpora spanning English, French, and German» (comprehension) and «over 200 single-child corpora representing 29 languages» (production) [opened both]. **Production reuses comprehension's own chunks.**
- **Grammar by compression, with no neural network.** Stolcke & Omohundro 1994 (arXiv cmp-lg/9409010, abstract): «samples are incorporated by adding ad-hoc rules to a working grammar; subsequently, elements of the model (such as states or nonterminals) are merged to achieve generalization and a more compact representation. The choice of what to merge and when to stop is governed by the Bayesian posterior probability of the grammar given the data, which formalizes a trade-off between a close fit to the data and a default preference for simpler models ('Occam's Razor')». Solan, Horn, Ruppin & Edelman 2005, PNAS (ADIOS): «our unsupervised algorithm recursively distills from it hierarchically structured patterns … capable of learning complex syntax, generating grammatical novel sentences». Kolodny, Lotem & Edelman 2015, Cogn Sci: «incrementally learns a grammar that captures its statistical patterns, which can then be used to parse or generate new data. The grammar … takes the form of a directed weighted graph, whose nodes are recursively (hierarchically) defined patterns over the elements of the input stream» [opened all three]. These are the pure-road relatives of the Toy C compressor (§(d) L1).

### a.4 Producing a sentence: a pipeline with a monitor
- Levelt, Roelofs & Meyer 1999, BBS: «We generate them two or three per second in fluent conversation … After a first stage of conceptual preparation, word generation proceeds through lexical selection, morphological and phonological encoding, phonetic encoding, and articulation itself. In addition, the speaker exerts some degree of output control, by monitoring of self-produced internal and overt speech» [opened].
- The look leads the mouth. Griffin & Bock 2000, Psychol Sci: «response-relevant information was rapidly extracted from scenes, allowing speakers to select grammatical subjects based on comprehended events rather than salience. When speaking extemporaneously, speakers began fixating pictured elements less than a second before naming them within their descriptions, a finding consistent with incremental lexical encoding. Eye movements anticipated the order of mention despite changes in picture orientation, in who-did-what-to-whom, and in sentence structure» [opened]. (An INTERFACE with the organism's eye: the glance's four fixations and its coarse view are made first, and the turn's say is about the hypotheses grouped from them, `genome/brain.py:99–112, 528–561`: the look precedes the say within one canvas.)
- Production and comprehension are interwoven. Pickering & Garrod 2013, BBS: «actors construct forward models of their actions before they execute those actions … speakers and comprehenders use covert imitation and forward modeling to make predictions at these levels of representation … and (c) how they use these predictions to monitor the upcoming utterances» [opened]. Hickok 2012, Nat Rev Neurosci: «a neuroanatomically grounded, hierarchical state feedback control model of speech production» [opened].
- Dell 1986 (a spreading-activation theory of retrieval in sentence production) [record opened, text not] is the classic interactive-activation account of slips [from memory]; Levelt 1983 (monitoring and self-repair) [record opened, text not] is the perceptual-loop theory [from memory].

### a.5 Monitoring one's own speech — two mechanisms, both documented
- **Through comprehension (the perceptual loop).** Postma 2000, Cognition (a review of three theories): «The perception-based approach proposes that the same mechanism employed in understanding other-produced language, the speech comprehension system, is also used to monitor one's own speech production … From the empirical evidence presently available, it is argued for a central perception-based monitor, potentially augmented with a few automatic, production-based error detection devices» [opened].
- **Through conflict inside production.** Nozari, Dell & Schwartz 2011, Cogn Psychol: «error detection is accomplished by monitoring response conflict via a frontal brain structure, such as the anterior cingulate cortex … Our results show a strong correlation between patients' error-detection ability and the model's characterization of their production skills, and no significant correlation between error detection and comprehension measures, thus supporting a production-based monitor» (aphasic patients) [opened]. The organism already holds a number of this kind: the MARGIN between the best and the second candidate (b, `produce_believed`).
- **What this licenses:** the organism may use BOTH (a read-back through its own parse, and a conflict margin), and the two are separable experimentally (a plant of known errors; (g)). It may not claim either is «the» human mechanism: the literature disagrees (Postma 2000 vs Nozari 2011).

### a.6 Inner speech
- Alderson-Day & Fernyhough 2015, Psychol Bull: «Inner speech — also known as covert speech or verbal thinking — has been implicated in theories of cognitive development, speech monitoring, executive function, and psychopathology … inner speech appears to perform significant functions in human cognition, which in some cases reflect its developmental origins and its sharing of resources with other cognitive processes» [opened].
- It is production without the last stages. Oppenheim & Dell 2008, Cognition: «While lexical bias was present in both inner and overt speech errors, the phonemic similarity effect was evident only for overt errors … inner speech is impoverished at lower (featural) levels, but robust at higher (phonemic) levels» [opened]. (Words and their order survive the loss of articulation; the sound does not.)
- Its sensory content comes from a prediction. Scott, Yeung, Gick & Werker 2013, J Acoust Soc Am: «the auditory content of inner speech is provided by corollary discharge. Corollary discharge is the motor system's prediction of the sensory consequences of its actions. This prediction can bias perception of other sensations» [opened]. (So hearing one's own voice is not new evidence about the world: a design fact for the ear, (d) L4.)
- It develops from talk to others, to talk to oneself, to thought: Winsler 2003 (table) [opened]; Vygotsky's account [from memory].

### a.7 Talking about oneself
- **A self-concept in stages.** Rochat 2003, Conscious Cogn: «5 levels of self-awareness are presented and discussed as they chronologically unfold from the moment of birth to approximately 4–5 years of age» [opened]. CDC 30 months: «Says words like "I," "me," or "we"»; 3 years: «Says first name, when asked» (table).
- **Self-reference has two forms and the CDC does not order them.** It lists «I, me, we» at 30 months and «Says first name, when asked» at 3 years — two different skills (a pronoun used, a question answered with one's name), so the pages establish no order between referring to oneself by name and by pronoun. The pronoun-reversal error («you» said for oneself, learned from speech addressed to the child) is Charney 1980 and Oshima-Takane 1988 [records opened, text not; [from memory]: children who hear speech between OTHER people, not addressed to them, learn I/you correctly; those who only hear addressed speech reverse them; and toddlers often name themselves before they say «I»].
- **Talk about the mind is taught by talk about the mind.** Meins et al. 2002, Child Dev: «mothers' use of appropriate mental state comments independently predicted overall ToM performance, accounting for 11% of the variance» (comments at 6 months, tasks at 45 and 48 months). Ruffman, Slade & Crowe 2002, Child Dev: «Mothers' use of mental state utterances in these descriptions at early time points was consistently correlated with later theory-of-mind understanding … Mothers' mental state utterances seemed genuinely causal because early theory-of-mind ability was not related to later mother mental state utterances … Results also showed that children's desire talk preceded their talk about beliefs» [opened both]. (Mental verbs, Shatz et al. 1983, [record opened, text not; [from memory] «want» before «think» and «know», across the third year].)
- **Uncertainty is reported early, nonverbally first** (Goupil 2016, table): the child that «asks for help» at 20 months says «I don't know» in action months before it says it in words.
- **A life story is built with an adult's help.** Nelson & Fivush 2004, Psychol Rev: autobiographical memory «emerges gradually across the preschool years. The components that contribute … include basic memory abilities, language and narrative, adult memory talk, temporal understanding, and understanding of self and others» [opened].

### a.8 Conversation and contingency
- Turn-taking: Stivers et al. 2009, PNAS: «a general avoidance of overlapping talk and a minimization of silence between conversational turns … within a range of 250 ms from the cross-language mean» [opened]. CDC 3 years: two back-and-forth exchanges.
- **Contingent speech teaches; prerecorded speech does not.** Goldstein & Schwade 2008, Psychol Sci (9.5-month-olds): «Infants given contingent feedback rapidly restructured their babbling, incorporating phonological patterns from caregivers' speech, but infants given noncontingent feedback did not». Kuhl, Tsao & Liu 2003, PNAS: «exposure to recorded Mandarin, without interpersonal interaction, had no effect … infants show phonetic learning from live, but not prerecorded, exposure to a foreign language». Hirsh-Pasek et al. 2015, Psychol Sci: the quality of early parent–child communication at 24 months «accounted for 27% of the variance in expressive language 1 year later … considerably more potent predictors … than was the quantity of mothers' words» [opened all].

### a.9 What the human data say about the DESIGN (the seven lines (d) is built on)
1. **Comprehension first.** Words are understood at 6 months, said at about 12; a mouth is built after, and out of, the ear's road (Pickering & Garrod; McCauley & Christiansen).
2. **Early sentences are schemas with a slot**, almost never a free combination; productivity is limited at 2 and grows at 3 (Lieven; Bannard). **A grammar of rules is not the first thing to build.**
3. **A frame is found where its middle varies** (Gómez; Mintz; Onnis): the learner needs variation sets, i.e. the same sentence with one element changed, close together in the speech it hears.
4. **A message precedes the words** (Levelt: conceptual preparation first), and **the eye leads the mouth** (Griffin & Bock).
5. **Two monitors** (Postma; Nozari): a read-back through comprehension and a conflict signal inside production.
6. **Inner speech is the same pipeline minus articulation**, its content a prediction that is not mistaken for the world (Oppenheim & Dell; Scott).
7. **Self-talk is learned from talk about oneself that is true and contingent** (Meins; Ruffman; Goldstein & Schwade; Kuhl); **uncertainty is reported before it is said** (Goupil); **the pronoun is the hard part of self-reference** (Charney, Oshima-Takane [from memory]), which is why the design below starts from a name (a design choice, not a claim about the child's order).

### b.2 How the brain's mouth works (the floor a language core must beat)
1. **A word is a typed prototype.** The brain learns, per scene, the words heard against the things in view by a delta rule (`genome/brain.py:309–347`, `RWLearner.train_scene`); its TYPES come from an EXCLUSION graph: words that never occur in one utterance are one kind (`brain/scene/learners.py:198–210`, `Ear.cover`: the largest maximal clique of the never-together graph, greedily). [STATUS] `brain.types`: **shapes** (arrow, bar, circle, cross, diamond, dot, ring, square, star, triangle, wave), **colours** (blue, green, red, white, yellow), **sizes** (big, small).
2. **Word order is a per-word NUMBER**, the running mean of its relative position in the utterances of length ≥ 2 that carry it (`learners.py:148–186`, `Ear.key`, `Ear.order`); a phrase is its words sorted by that key. There is no constant, no slot, no hierarchy: the grammar of the brain is «one word of each kind, kinds in key order».
3. **`produce_believed`** (`learners.py:1187–1244`): per type the believed name of each thing; a candidate is a non-empty SUBSET of the sayable believed names of the target; it must be believed-UNIQUE (no other thing has the same names) and its READ-BACK — the learner's own pointing, `answer_point` — must put the target first with «none» not firing; the spoken phrase is the SHORTEST accepted, ties by read-back margin; else «I cannot say it yet» with one reason. **It never samples a vocabulary and it never reads the words already said:** it is a selection among ≤ 7 subsets, not a next-token model. The tie generator is a named substream (`genome/brain.py:549`).
4. **The mouth has two monitors already**: the read-back (the perceptual loop's shape) and the MARGIN (`learners.py:1232–1234`: score of the target minus the best other, +∞ with one thing in view; the conflict signal's shape).
5. **It can refuse a nonsense phrase** (`Brain.misuse`, `genome/brain.py:447–461`: two words of one learned kind in one phrase, «a red blue») and explain a word from its own episodes (`Brain.why`, `:468–498`).
6. **The parent it learns from speaks four templates and no verb** (`brain/scene/world.py:142–150`): T1 «⟨shape⟩», T2 «⟨colour⟩ ⟨shape⟩», T3 «⟨size⟩ ⟨shape⟩», T4 «⟨size⟩ ⟨colour⟩ ⟨shape⟩», each ¼, the thing uniform among the things in view; **its entropy is 6.0774 bits an utterance** (the Toy C law's §0, from the generator's constants).

## (d) Mechanisms proposed

**The shape in one paragraph.** A sentence is built the way the human data say it is (a.9). A MESSAGE comes first (L2). A CONSTRUCTION the creature has heard and kept carries it (L1, L3). The brain's existing phrase mouth fills the noun-phrase slot (L3 calls `produce_believed` unchanged). The result is READ BACK through the same store that understands speech and CHECKED for conflict before it is said (L3). When nothing is said aloud, the same pipeline is inner speech (L4). Talking is the pipeline with a heard question as its message (L5). Nothing here needs a new kind of learner: the learners are the ones already in the body — a delta rule over words and things, and (Toy C) a compressor that keeps what shortens held-out text. The only new object is a **store of constructions**. What no sentence can do without is in L6: **a world that speaks sentences** (gap 2).

**The pure-road audit — the compile's refusal test, written so it can be run as a test and not argued.** For every mechanism: (i) what is LEARNED; (ii) from WHAT — only heard utterances and the organism's own signals; (iii) no weight, table, lexicon or embedding comes from outside the life; (iv) no step defines, scores or samples a distribution over the vocabulary conditioned on the words already said. (iv) is a test in (e): **SLOT-INDEPENDENCE, with a planted offender.** Named and refused here: a small language model as a «surface realiser» or «sentence smoother», a pretrained tokenizer or sentence embedding, a model that is trained to predict the next word given a message (the human-faithful connectionist rival is exactly that, [from memory] Chang, Dell & Bock 2006 — not opened, not adopted).

### L1 · SCHEMA — the store of constructions and the compressor that keeps them (extends Toy C)
- **What it is.** A construction is a sequence of tokens, each a CONSTANT (a heard word) or a SLOT of a kind (one of the brain's learned types, or a distributional class), plus a MEANING record (L6), a support count, and `gain`: the bits it saves on held-out heard utterances, net of its own description (Toy C §1: `G(F) = bits(held-out | ∅) − bits(held-out | F) − L(F)`, with a slotted construction coded as one token plus its filler under the slot's own counts).
- **How it is learned — each step is a human mechanism of (a):**
  1. REMEMBER heard utterances whole, as schemas with no slot (Tomasello 2000: concrete expressions first).
  2. ABSTRACT by ONE-OPERATION SUBSTITUTION (Lieven 2003): two stored utterances that differ in exactly one position, the two words being of one kind, become one schema with a slot of that kind. Where the kind is unknown the slot opens a candidate class, found by the FRAME the words share (Mintz 2003: the words that stand between the same two constants); VARIATION SETS (Onnis 2008, L6 R2) make it visible sooner.
  3. KEEP by HELD-OUT COMPRESSION: a schema stays only while `gain > 0` on the held-out 40 % of the recent utterances; otherwise pruned. This is his own notes' rule (RESEARCH-2026-10-03 §0 row 2: «frames with slots … pruned when they stop paying off») and the lifelong grow-and-prune cycle of the map (B10).
  4. MERGE CLASSES by posterior (Stolcke & Omohundro 1994): two slot classes merge when the merged model's description length is lower. An optional step for later rungs.
- **When.** At SLEEP, the body's own consolidation (`SLEEP every 2500 moments`, [genome :68]; [STATUS] `sleeps 124`), never on the canvas path.
- **Interface.** READS the ear's utterances in order with their source and moment (`words_in_instant`, stored per episode, `genome/fast.py:81–88, 142`, and read the way `find_frames` reads it, `genome/sentence.py:55–62`), `Brain.types()` (brain.py:441–445), `Brain.census()` (only MATURE words may fill a slot: `mature` is the census flag, brain.py:274–275). WRITES `state/schema.json` and one journal row a sleep (`kind: schema`: constructions born and pruned, their gains, the held-out size). It is a reader and a store, never a second writer of memory (his 09-17 invariants: one owner of memory; the replay gate).
- **Gene.** `SCHEMA induce at sleep; held-out 0.4; keep when gain > 0; slots by kind` — a parser edit in `genome/machinery.py`, which is in `cache.WORLD_CODE`: **the pin's price** (SOLUTIONS #245 (1); the INNER landing 141ce30 paid it): a fresh run root for the scene caches and an array-by-array identity check with a planted bit. Every value of the line is read by named code (no VALUE-UNREAD, the discipline of brain.py:26–39).
- **Toy C is its first instrument.** The compressor, the nulls (dealt frames, random ADJACENT bigrams of the same count), the plant and the SCENE ceiling are built, tested and parked (resume command on the board); L1's organ compressor is a second implementation tested against the toy's slow recount to 1e-9, as the toy's own was.
- **Pure-road audit.** (i) the store; (ii) heard utterances; (iii) nothing outside; (iv) a slot's filler class is a SET with counts used to PARSE and to bound production, and the compressor is a coding MEASURE, not a generator.

### L2 · MESSAGE — the Intent record, its producers, the reasons to speak
- **What it is.** Before any word, the speaker holds an `Intent` {kind: REPORT | ANSWER | ASK | REFUSE; subject: SELF | WORLD; predicate: SEE | KNOW | NOT-KNOW | THINK | DO; args: a thing hypothesis, a word, an act record; reason: why now; evidence: the row it was read from}. Levelt: conceptual preparation precedes lexical selection; Griffin & Bock: the message is built from the comprehended event. It is journaled WITH the utterance, so the grader can compare what was said with what was meant.
- **Three producers, all READING records that exist** (this part defines no new knowledge of the self; that is R-SELF's):
  1. PERCEPT — the DRIVE turn's thing and its believed names (`Brain.turn`: `say`, `expected`, `held`, brain.py:505–561) → REPORT / SEE.
  2. EPISTEMIC — the same turn's doubt (`held` empty, the `none` flag, the margin) and, from the self, the per-word reliability (`SelfNode.reliability`, web.py:429) and the census's `mature` / `young` → KNOW / NOT-KNOW. **The interface to R-SELF is one call:** `self_view()` returns facts that are TRUE BY RECORD, each with a reference to the row that makes it true; what the self holds, and what its census counts, is R-SELF's design.
  3. THOUGHT — the INNER turn's row (`act: inner`: topic, topic_source, the remembered scene's say, surprise; mind.py:1796–1799) → THINK(topic), reason = topic_source.
- **The reasons to speak are a policy, replacing the DRIVE's `says 1` (every canvas):** speak when (a) ADDRESSED (L5); (b) the canvas is SURPRISING (`Brain.surprise`, brain.py:601–609, above a floor: Liszkowski 2004, pointing to share); (c) an epistemic state CHANGES (a claim is demoted; a word crosses `young` → `mature`: news to the listener); (d) a DOUBT is to be shared (the existing ask; Goupil 2016). A TOLD-SET (what was said about which thing or state in the last W canvases) suppresses repeats: tell the one who does not know (Liszkowski 2008). The cells' mouth and the brain's say keep their own rows as the floor speakers.
- **Gene.** `SPEAK reasons addressed surprise change doubt; told window 20`.
- **Pure-road audit.** An Intent holds the organism's OWN signals (a percept, a flag, a journaled topic) and no words; its predicates are genome-level conceptual primitives, stated as priors like the self's claim quantifiers `always | usually` (web.py Claim), not learned from text. **A decision for the seat, not hidden:** the list SEE / KNOW / NOT-KNOW / THINK / DO is OURS.

### L3 · THE MOUTH BY CONSTRUCTION, with its two monitors
1. **SELECT** the constructions whose learned meaning unifies with the Intent (predicate, polarity, argument arity) and whose slots the Intent's arguments can fill with MATURE words. The most SPECIFIC wins (the elsewhere condition); ties by `gain`, then by name; an exact tie by a named substream, as `produce_believed` does (brain.py:549).
2. **FILL.** A thing-NP slot is filled BY `produce_believed`, unchanged: the brain's mouth becomes the noun-phrase formulator inside a sentence, and its read-back, margin and refusal reasons come with it. A word slot takes the Intent's word.
3. **LINEARISE** by the construction's own token order. There is no score over orderings of a vocabulary.
4. **MONITOR** — both a.5 mechanisms: **(i) CONFLICT**: the margin between the best and the runner-up construction and between the best and the runner-up filler (the number `produce_believed` already returns, learners.py:1232–1234); an Intent whose margin is under a floor is not said. **(ii) READ-BACK**: the produced sentence is PARSED by the same store that parses a heard one (McCauley & Christiansen: «the same chunks and statistics involved during comprehension»); the recovered Intent is compared with the intended one slot by slot, the NP slot by `answer_point` (the existing check). An unequal read is a REPAIR — the next candidate, at most k = 3 — and then «I cannot say it yet», with a reason (the existing ones plus «no construction», «form», «slot»).
5. **SPEAK**: one `say` journal row with words, construction id, Intent, read-back and margin.
- **It must not break:** the cells' frame mouth (kept as the floor speaker BY NAME, as STAGE-2 part C says); the brain's scored answers (the NP road is untouched; a JOIN CHECK on the 2,209 / 2,222 / 2,221 answers, as every part since BRAIN-IN-BODY ran it).
- **Floors to beat:** `produce_believed` alone (the bare phrase) and `genome/chain.py` (the cells' chained frames). The new mouth must not lose truth against the bare phrase on the same canvases (a paired reading) and must be a sentence where they are not.
- **Pure-road audit.** SELECT is unification over finite sets; FILL is the brain's existing selection; LINEARISE is a stored order; MONITOR reads and compares. The SLOT-INDEPENDENCE test (e.0) is the proof of (iv).

### L4 · INNER SPEECH — the same pipeline without its last step, and the self-voice rule
1. **COVERT SAY.** L2 → L3 with articulation off: an `inner_say` row, no `say` row. The INNER turn's one-phrase says become construction sentences once L3 exists; the walk that picks the next thought (creature/inner.py:30–70) is unchanged.
2. **UTTERANCE BUFFER.** The last 3 heard and 3 own utterances (covert or overt) in a small ring inside the mind: the working-memory loop that the map's B9 says is absent («None. One item per think»). A two-turn exchange (L5) and rehearsal both need it. It holds WORDS — Oppenheim & Dell: inner speech is robust at the word level and impoverished below it, and the organism's utterances are word-level.
3. **THE SELF-VOICE RULE.** An utterance the organism made, covert or overt, reaches its own learners only as a MONITOR input (the read-back), never as a training example. The ear tags any ear row equal to a recent own utterance `self`; the brain's learner and the schema learner skip it. This is the corollary-discharge analogue: a predicted consequence is not evidence about the world (Scott 2013). Today it is harmless (`voice_rows 0`, its says are journal rows); it is written now because the pure road dies the day the creature's own sentences become its teacher.
4. **REHEARSAL, idle only** (the INNER condition: a body row with backlog 0): covert says of remembered scenes are scored by the monitor; a construction whose read-back keeps failing loses `gain` standing at the next sleep (monitoring and executive function: Alderson-Day & Fernyhough).
- **Reads** the INNER row, the buffer, the monitor. **Writes** `inner_say` rows and the ring (inside the snapshot GENERATION the days law requires, mind.py `snapshot`).
- **Pure-road audit.** The self-voice rule IS the audit of this mechanism: without it, a closed loop; with it, an identity (e.1, L4 mark).

### L5 · SPEECH ACTS AND TURNS — answering, asking, offering
- A heard question is parsed by L1 (comprehension) into an Intent kind: «what do you see?» → REPORT/SEE; «do you know this?» → KNOW/NOT-KNOW; «what are you thinking of?» → THINK; «why?» → the reason of the last Intent (the buffer). The answer is L3's. The ASK stays the doubt's act (10 % of canvases today); the new part is that an ask is a SENTENCE and is answered by the world door.
- **Turns in a mind-paced world:** the reply is journaled at the canvas boundary that follows the question, before the next heard row. The human gap (Stivers 2009: no overlap, no long silence) becomes: exactly ONE reply per question, at the next boundary.
- **Interface.** The CHAT door is the road for a LIFE and is **HIS decision** (RULES ADDENDUM 20:18). Until his word L5 is benched on closed copies through the EXAM protocol, whose asks are never learned from («an ask never teaches», brain.py:362–363), with new ask forms. Reads the buffer; writes the answer row.
- **Pure-road audit.** A question is parsed by the same store; there is no scripted dialogue table.

### L6 · THE PARENT'S SIDE — what the world must speak for any of this to be learnable (a request to R-PERCEPTION)
- **R1 CARRIER SENTENCES.** A constant frame round the noun phrase, with the organism's NAME and a verb: «NAME sees ⟨NP⟩». The four templates (brain/scene/world.py:142–150) become the NP slot's content; every sentence TRUE by construction from the world's truth files (`truth/scene/<seq>.json`, the INNER bench's own cross-check, A2.2).
- **R2 VARIATION SETS** (Onnis 2008; Gómez 2002): runs of ≥ 3 utterances that share the carrier and change ONE position — in the scene world, several utterances over ONE canvas about its different things.
- **R3 JOINT ATTENTION** (Tomasello & Farrar 1986): the sentence names what the organism is LOOKING AT (its `fix` cells), and does not redirect it.
- **R4 CONTINGENT MIRROR SPEECH** (Goldstein & Schwade 2008; Kuhl 2003; Meins 2002; Ruffman 2002). For S2 and S3: statements ABOUT THE ORGANISM generated from its OWN journal within one canvas of the act or state — «NAME does not know this» after a wrong forced name or a `none`; «NAME thinks ring» from an `inner` row. Never prerecorded; never about anything it has not done. **A fact for the seat and for him:** this narrator is the seat's templates, a teacher's voice, as the scene parent is. Every word is HEARD, so the pure road holds; but the organism's sentences carry that grammar until richer speech arrives through the shelf and the doors (L7).
- **R5 NON-ADDRESSED SPEECH** (later; **NO WORLD today**, #228 (a)): for «I / you» (Oshima-Takane 1988, Charney 1980 [from memory]): the parent speaks to a second software partner in the organism's hearing.
- **R6 A POLARITY PAIR:** «sees» / «does not see», so that negation is grounded in the brain's own refusal (`none`, test 2a: 0.868 / 0.810 / 0.892, CHILD-LADDER L22).
- **THE MEANING SIDE — how it is learned.** A construction's meaning is its association with the organism's OWN signals at the moments it was heard — the SITUATION: what it fixated, its doubt flag, its last act and outcome, its last inner topic. This is cross-situational learning (Smith & Yu 2008) by the delta-rule association the brain already uses for words, with a STATE VECTOR as a second kind of «thing». **This is the one piece with no code to extend** (the brain's learner takes 144-number visual hypotheses, brain.py:99–112): it is the first toy (g, T-L4).

### L7 · GROWTH TOWARD ENGLISH, MATH AND SCIENCE with the same organ (stated for the interface; the other lanes own the inputs)
1. **English.** Constructions induced from the SHELF (ungrounded text) are QUARANTINED patterns with no meaning side (CONCLUSION §2: «books add heard-of words and patterns, never meaning»): they may PARSE, but the mouth asserts only constructions with a learned meaning and only mature words. A quarantined pattern is ALIGNED when a grounded utterance matches its constants — one-shot, by Lieven's one-operation substitution (a toy question, g).
2. **Words that cannot be pointed at** (because, not, more, every) are learned as construction CONSTANTS whose meaning is an OPERATION on the organism's own record: «not» ↔ the brain's refusal (`none`); «because» ↔ `Brain.why` and the inner row's `topic_source`; «more / every» ↔ census comparisons. Each needs R-SELF's or R-MATHSCI's operation named; their truth is COMPUTED by an examiner from the record, never judged by a language model.
3. **Math and science.** A numeral is grounded by COUNTING acts (R-MATHSCI); an equation is a construction with numeric slots whose truth the examiner computes. Causal verbs are grounded by the hands' outcomes (`touch`: exit, rate, stalls; [STATUS] `runs 843`) and by INDUCE's filed rules (321 in the 24-hour alpha4 life, map §4): «when NAME runs ⟨program⟩ it ends quickly» is a construction over a rule.
4. **The vocabulary beyond 24 words** comes through the shelf and the lexicon door's glosses (a definition is a sentence made of known words): R-PERCEPTION's road.
