## 1 · The human mechanism it answers (and what it does not claim)

- **One mouth, fed by meaning.** STAGE-2 part C cites Hickok & Poeppel 2007 [as the design cites it; I did not open it]. The organism today has TWO speakers that never meet: a mouth fed by co-occurrence and a brain fed by meaning.
- **A child's first words are about what it attends to** (Tomasello & Farrar 1986, abstract opened in RESEARCH-LANGUAGE §a.1): the brain's phrase names the thing under its gaze.
- **Early speech is made of whole concrete items** (Tomasello 2000, body read in the final pass): the brain's phrase is a word, or two words that pick the thing out (9,212 of 9,240 says are one word). Nothing here asks for a sentence.
- **Not claimed:** a model of speech production; «about itself» beyond what it SEES (S0's content is what the seat's list of self-facts calls «what it has seen»; S1 is the first sentence with the organism as its subject, W3); understanding; a reason to speak (the DRIVE says on 0.943 of canvases, because its line says `says 1`); an answer (no world answers).
- **The pure road holds:** the voiced phrase is the brain's own selection among the subsets of the words it believes (`produce_believed`, never next-token); the mouth adds no learner, no table, no distribution over the vocabulary.

## 5 · Adversarial walk

1. **Where can it fire?** Only on a canvas the brain hears and whose turn says something: 0.947–0.951 of the canvases after the first calibration; never before it (`reason: not calibrated`), never with nothing in view; under `says 0` it fires nowhere (a unit test). On the maker's canvases (`hears scene maker`) it fires too; those rows are COUNTED here and graded in a later life.
2. **T is not an independent claim, and this law says so.** Because V makes a voice row equal its turn row, T on the lean lives is stage −1's table again (0.978 / 0.974 / 0.979) and the DRIVE's never-used-world reading (0.972) is the same mouth's. T's job is to be the gate a wrong wiring fails, not a new finding about truth. The finding W1 adds is V and the identities: the body speaks the brain's phrase and nothing else moved.
3. **The ablated system on T's own items (#246 (1)).** A mouth that echoes the parent's words scores about 1.0 on T and is caught only by V (control 2); the dealt phrases read ≤ 0.211; the constant phrase ≈ 0.2 and fails the absolute bar (Z); the planted say 0.000; the mute mouth passes T vacuously and fails C. Each of the four fails a DIFFERENT mark, which is the point of having them all.
4. **Every «every / first / by N» counted against the record.** «Every canvas» (V) = 4 × 10,000 with the presence counts of the turn rows carrying a say and of the voice rows; «the lowest window» (C) = ten windows of 1,000 per life; «the first true word» is a reading.
5. **Order.** The voice is written after the turn and before the learn, like the turn, so it cannot read the heard words; the order plant proves the grader would see the reverse.
6. **The self-voice rule.** A voiced phrase is a journal row. It is never an ear row and never a learner's input; I2 proves the brain's learned state and the cells' frame rows are equal with the voice on, on four lives.
7. **A mark that passes by silence.** The cells' mouth passes its own test by saying nothing (mind.py:3888–3889 says so); T is therefore paired with C and Z.
8. **The pin.** The clause's regex and key are the only parser change; the old spelling's dict is pinned unchanged; the redraw and its planted bit are the window's gate (§2).
9. **Cost and growth.** One row a canvas is the only new write; K bounds the CPU; the journal grows by the figure in §4.
10. **Two mouths on one canvas.** On the full life a canvas may carry the frame mouth's `say` row and the brain's `voice` row. They are different rows from different gates (the frame mouth's per think, the voice's per canvas); F proves the frame rows and counters equal the OFF replay's, so the voice cannot have changed what the frame mouth says.
11. **What the replays cannot see.** The closed lives are scene worlds; the maker's phrases and a life with `hears scene maker` are not graded by T. The first life to carry the clause is graded again on its own journal (a reading).

## 6 · What this law does NOT give, and what would

- **No sentence, no «NAME».** S1 («NAME sees ⟨thing⟩») needs the mentor's carrier sentences (W2), the intent and the schema store (W3). This law gives the floor S1's TRUE is read against: the sentence mouth must not fall below the bare phrase on the same canvases (RESEARCH-LANGUAGE §e.1).
- **No reason to speak.** The voice follows the DRIVE's `says 1`: every canvas that has a say. The told-set, surprise, state change and doubt policy are L2's and later.
- **Only the live say is voiced.** The brain's ASK stays a journal row (a question to a world that answers no one) and the INNER turn's say stays a thought (a journal row, never voiced): the line between speaking and thinking aloud is W4's, not this law's.
- **No answer, no door.** Nobody reads a voice row but the graders. The world does not answer; the mentor (W2) is a world, not a part of this law. Whether the mentor reads the journal or the door is W2's decision.
- **No learning from its own voice.** The closed loop is refused; a rung that wants to learn from its own say is a new law with the self-voice rule's identity as its gate.
- **A life.** W1 enters a life only as the ONE advancement over its twin (RULES §3), after its bench is read and after the window lands it.
