## §2 · Does the human brain do that? And how does IT form content?

**Yes — at the population level it is the same two-substance-free picture: content lives as changed
synaptic efficacy across assemblies of neurons** (the engram line; our R2 from last night — real
assemblies delineated by mutual inhibition). The brain's content-formation machinery, in order:

1. **Encoding**: synaptic plasticity (LTP/LTD) at the synapses of co-active neurons — the Hebbian core.
2. **Salience selection**: **synaptic tagging and capture** (Frey & Morris 1997's root; Redondo & Morris
   2011, cited 1,113; Dunsmoor et al. 2022, cited 101) — a WEAK learning event is stabilized only if a
   STRONG (salient/surprising/rewarding) event happens nearby in time; the tag catches the plasticity
   proteins the strong event triggers. The brain does NOT keep everything — it keeps what mattered near
   what mattered. (This is the biological form of our drives-tagged consolidation, §4 D4.)
3. **Two-speed consolidation**: the hippocampus writes fast, detailed, index-like traces; the neocortex
   learns slowly, integrating replays into general knowledge (McClelland-McNaughton-O'Reilly 1995, the
   CLS paper; Squire's standard model; the 2026 reviews agree). Our FAST episodes → web consolidation +
   sleep replay is this form at the system level.
4. **Remodeling by birth**: adult dentate-gyrus **neurogenesis** keeps transforming detailed memories
   into more general ones during systems consolidation (PMC13000687, 2026 review) — newborn cells are
   not just added capacity; they CHANGE old content's form. Reconsolidation: recalled memories become
   editable again and are re-stored (the belief-chain's biological warrant, Ahmad's 10-11 directive).

## §3 · How the human brain ITSELF is formed (the body's formation — the deeper half of his question)

The genome does not store a brain's content; it stores the RULES that build a machine which then stores
content through experience. Two formation processes, always separate:

1. **The body's formation (genetic, mostly one-time):** progenitor pools (neuroepithelial cells → radial
   glia) divide — first symmetrically (more founders), then asymmetrically (founder + neuron); neurons
   MIGRATE along radial-glia fibers; the cortical sheet is laid **inside-out** (layer VI first, II last —
   the radial-unit hypothesis, Rakic's line; 2025's subcellular radial-glia transcriptomes and 2026's
   neurovascular-interface work keep sharpening it). Then the overproduction-and-selection economy:
   **exuberant synaptogenesis to a peak (~age 2–3), then synaptic PRUNING into adolescence** (Huttenlocher
   & Dabholkar's classic curve; 2026's bioRxiv microglia-pruning trajectories; and 2026-01's teen-brain
   finding: pruning AND active building together), myelination into the twenties, and **critical/sensitive
   periods** — windows where experience sculpts the scaffold with extra plasticity, then the window closes.
2. **The content's formation (plastic, lifelong):** §2's machinery, running ON that scaffold.

**The deep engineering answer to his question is therefore NOT a better neuron copy — it is FORMATION
ENGINEERING: the rules that build the tissue and the rules that write the content, as two explicit
processes.** The exact-neuron-copy road (compartmental models) is heavy because it copies the wrong
abstraction level — a neuron is not the unit of content; the ASSEMBLY and the PLASTIC WEIGHT are. Millions
of copied neurons buy nothing that assemblies + plasticity + formation rules do not buy cheaper.
