## 1 · The human mechanism it answers (the map's rows, with tags)

- **B1 — the brain is active by itself** («brain functions are mainly intrinsic», Raichle 2010 [opened 09-29, the map]). Today the brain moves only on `learn` and `ask` and its DRIVE turn on a live canvas.
- **B4 — two stores and replay** (Kumaran, Hassabis, McClelland 2016 [opened 09-29, the map]): the episodic store is read back. Today nothing reads the brain's stored scenes but the `why` exam.
- **B9 — a small working memory**: the chain's last thoughts (the inhibition-of-return list, two words) are the first state the brain carries from one moment to the next outside its learning. Today none.
- **Spreading activation** (Collins & Loftus 1975 [from memory, as the reading cited it]) and **memory search as a random walk on a semantic network** (Abbott, Austerweil & Griffiths 2015; Hills, Jones & Todd 2012 — «optimal foraging» in recall [from memory, not opened today]): recall moves from a cue to its associates and leaves a patch it has exhausted. The seeded random walk with inhibition of return is the cheapest honest form of both; the greedy walk of §0.2 is the form recall is known NOT to take.

Not answered here, and not claimed: consolidation (learning from the remembered scene), meaning beyond «true of the thing», a sentence.

**Why the null deals PHRASES, not scenes.** Dealing scenes breaks the target's position: almost no thing of another scene lies under the target, so a constant mouth that keeps its target would beat that null by the eye's skill alone — the ablated system would pass, and the mark would test the eye, not the memory. Dealing phrases keeps the eye's part and removes only what the brain said of the thing.

**Readings (no bar; each prints its n, presence counts and, where paired, the smallest passing gap 10 / n):**
- **R-RET · retention, paired:** for each inner turn, the LIVE turn's say on the same scene when it was learned (the life's journal; DRIVE ran in `brain-long-2` from its first canvas) graded by the same rule: inner true vs live true, discordant counts both ways, 10 / n beside. In a stationary world the inner turn re-sees TRAINING scenes and should be at least as true; in a drifting world it is the instrument of forgetting.
- **R-AGE · the organism's own forgetting curve:** K's truth rate by deciles of the remembered scene's learn order (oldest to newest). Flat is the prediction on `brain-long-2`'s copy (one stationary world); its slope in a later life is the reading INNER-2 needs (§6).
- **R-ASSOC · does the association hold in memory:** the share of web hops (chain positions 2–5) whose re-seen say contains the topic the web chose, against a frequency-matched null (the topic replaced by a mature word of matched hearing count, ≥ 200 draws). Predicted near its null except for the few pairs whose lift departs from 1 (§0.3).
- **R-SELF · does the self know its weak words:** across the 13 words, Spearman's ρ between the self's lived reliability and the inner turns' per-word truth, with a label-permutation null (≥ 1,000 draws). A reading: the two are measured on related data, so agreement is expected and proves little; DISagreement would be the finding.

## 5 · Adversarial walk

1. **Where can the mechanism fire?** Not on any closed tape after the first calibration (§0.4: one eligible row of 40 on the full cut, none on the lean): G0 / G0b are identity checks and are SAID to be. In the bench, at every synthetic row. In a life, from the end of its scene stream (the queue is held at 8–13 while it trains), about once a minute after that.
2. **A bar above its ceiling?** K's bar is a percentile of its own null, so it has no absolute ceiling; its reachable maximum is the share of turns with a say whose target lies on a thing (printed beside: the say rate and the graded share). The live DRIVE turn says on ≈ 0.95 of canvases (the reading's turn series), so K has room.
3. **Ablated systems on the mark's own items.** K-ablated (a constant phrase, target kept) must fail; the dealt-phrase null is the same ablation drawn 200 times; R-ASSOC's frequency-matched topic is the ablated walk.
4. **Every «first / every / by N» counted.** «every body row when idle» = every body row with backlog 0, ONE turn each (G1); «restart 5» = the chain positions 1–5; «inhibit 2» = the last 2 topics; «13 words» = the mature words of the copy (dax excluded, 1 meeting); «1,000 turns» = 200 chains.
5. **The walk could ruminate.** Counted before this law (§0.2); G3 holds the walk to its coverage and makes the greedy rule a planted lock.
6. **The self chooses a weak word, so K is read on a skewed sample** (ring takes 0.24 of topics and is the least reliable word at 0.686). K's null is drawn on the same sample, so the bar stays fair; R-RET pairs every turn with its own live turn, so a low rate on ring scenes is read as the brain's weakness on rings, not as forgetting.
7. **Read-only means no improvement can be shown — and none is claimed.** The brain that re-sees a scene learns nothing from it; K and R-RET say whether its thoughts about its past are TRUE, R-AGE whether they stay true as the past recedes. «A self thought improving as it grows» needs a brain whose knowledge changes, then a turn that changes it — §6.
