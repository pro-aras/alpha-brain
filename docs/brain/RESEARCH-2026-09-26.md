## The three that matter for the brain

### 1. Self-Play Pretraining with Zero Data — arXiv 2609.30063 (September 2026) — read · **TEST**
- **What it does:** a generator proposes programs for a Brainf\*ck-like universal Turing machine. A learner predicts the program's output bytes. Both are Llama-style decoders; the runs go from a 1M-parameter learner up to 25M parameters at a 4K context.
- **The generator's reward:** r = |⟨∇θ L(y, θ_e), P_e ⊙ (θ_⌊e/2⌋ − θ_e)⟩|. The gradient a program causes is scored by how well it lines up with the learner's own recent movement. Mastered programs give almost no gradient. Unlearnable programs push in unrelated directions. Both earn little, so the generator lives at the learner's frontier.
- **Results:** zero-shot loss on natural data falls as a power law in self-play compute. The exponents are 0.066 for images, 0.123 for text and 0.249 for melody. In-context learning appears: reverse string, stack and associative recall reach close to 100 % with enough examples. Pretraining on a probabilistic grammar still beats it on text and code.
- **Its own limit:** «facts about a particular world must ultimately enter through interaction with that world.»
- **Why it matters here:** it is a road to give the brain general structure before birth without any borrowed text, so it fits D-1 and the pure road. The reward is also a principled lesson-picker for THE SCHOOL, where «harder each cycle» becomes «at the frontier, by gradient alignment». For the cells, which learn without gradients, the analogue is learning progress: the rate at which prediction error falls.
- **Test after B1 — an «algorithmic childhood»:** A1 pre-trained on self-play program data for a fixed CPU budget, against A1 from random initialisation, on B1's letters world. The question is whether the prior speeds learning and transfer.
- **Cost:** their budget ran to 34.36B tokens. Ours must be measured first, at ≤ 5M parameters and a few CPU hours.

### 2. GRAM, Generative Recursive Reasoning — arXiv 2605.19376 (May 2026; Baek, Jo, Kim, Ren, Bengio, Ahn) — read · **TEST**
- **What it does:** a 10M-parameter model thinks by iterating one shared step on a latent state. The high level updates once per step. The low level refines 4 to 6 times inside each step. A learned random nudge on the high level, trained by amortised variational inference, lets it hold several hypotheses. It can think deeper, with more steps, or wider, sampling up to 20 trajectories.
- **Results:**

| Benchmark | GRAM | TRM | HRM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sudoku-Extreme | 97.0 % | 87.4 % | 55.0 % |
| ARC-AGI-1 | 52.0 % | 44.6 % | 40.3 % |
| ARC-AGI-2 | 11.1 % | 7.8 % | 5.0 % |
| N-Queens 8×8, coverage with 20 samples | 90.3 % | 36.1 % | 26.7 % |

- **Training cost:** 8 RTX 4090 GPUs, 2 hours for Sudoku and 5 days for ARC.
- **Why it matters:** it is the most concrete working form of «a brain that thinks». Thinking means stepping a latent until it settles, with alternatives in parallel. Its parameter count fits this CPU. Its training compute does not.
- **Test at toy scale after B1:** his AND/OR/IF/THEN list as puzzles, such as truth tables, 4×4 Sudoku and 5-queens. A recursive head gets N inner steps, against the same head with one step. The question is whether iterating a latent solves what one pass cannot. It is a candidate «think» organ for the brain. It is not a B1 arm, because B1 is frozen.

### 3. JEPA-Anything — arXiv 2609.20800 (17 September 2026) — read (abstract) · **KEEP**
- **What it does:** it predicts in latent space instead of input space. The target is split into complementary factors, each learned by its own pathway, and recombined; the authors call this orthogonal predictive factorization.
- **Results:** it beats matched JEPA baselines on 10 of 10 dynamics tasks, and cuts single-intervention error on Interventional Pong by 34.8 %.
- **Why it matters:** CONCLUSION says the brain predicts its world. JEPA says predict the world's features, not its pixels. OPF says predict them as separate factors. Our organs already hand the mind factors: shape, position and colour traits. So when the brain's prediction head is built, one head per organ factor should be an arm against one joint head, in B2 or B3. Nothing to build now.

## From his PDF — three additions to the design, all small

- **The mouth reads back before it speaks** (PDF §11, back-translation) · **ADOPT, with his yes.** CONCLUSION law 6 already checks a sentence «against its own perception». Add a second check: the candidate sentence is read back through the comprehension road, and it is said only if it lands on the intended concept. It is cheap and pure. It also gives THE SCHOOL a new test: the brain says only what it can read back.
- **Confidence before the check** (PDF §12, reward prediction error) · **ADOPT in THE SCHOOL.** The brain records its confidence before every answer. Learning is scaled by surprise. THE SCHOOL grades calibration as the metacognition mark. B1's arms already carry an abstention band, so confidence becomes a first-class field on every answer row.
- **Backward transfer** (PDF §12) · **ADOPT as a reader field.** This is the literature's name for «never fall below a stage already reached». B2 measures retention; print BWT in the literature's form beside it.
- **Already in the design, no action:** recurrent logic gates are A2, which is the recurrent logic-gate network of PDF §9. Tsetlin clauses are A3. The rule library beside the grown network, combined in the hybrid arms, is the neuro-symbolic pairing of §10. Cells with sleep replay are complementary learning systems. The six marks cover §5 on understanding. Prediction error is the engine, as in §12. Growth under a regenerating budget answers §7 on overproduction and pruning. THE SCHOOL and the games cover §8's XLand.
- **One point to correct:** PDF §6 says to build concepts first on cheap data such as text, and ground them later. That conflicts with D-1, which says no borrowed text teaches the brain. Item 1 above is the pure-road form of that stage: structure from programs, facts from the world.
- **Not checked here:** the PDF's 2026 citations. These are the Ebbinghaus-curve forgetting paper, the self-consolidating language models, the provenance memory paper, the February belief-revision study and the June multilingual study.
