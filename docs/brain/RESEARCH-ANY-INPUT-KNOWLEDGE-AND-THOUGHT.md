## §1 · The question, decomposed

Two claims sit inside his correction: **(A)** knowledge intake is channel-free — any sense's input can
become knowledge/experience; **(B)** thought generation is not one fixed chain — it is open-ended, many
kinds of thoughts and questions. Both are answered by the literature — one fully supported with a
named bound, one supported and sharpened into a list of mechanisms.

## §2 · A — any input into knowledge: SUPPORTED, with one real bound

**The evidence that cortex is channel-agnostic compute:**

1. **The rewired ferret** (Sharma, Angelucci & Sur 2000, Nature 404:841; von Melchner, Pallas & Sur
   2000, Nature 404:871 — read via the critical review below + PubMed + the authors' own quotes):
   retinal axons diverted into the auditory thalamus of newborn ferrets → the AUDITORY cortex develops
   visual orientation maps and DRIVES VISUAL BEHAVIOUR (lesioning the rewired A1 abolishes the visual
   detection; lesioning the visual pathway does not). The tissue computes on the input's statistics,
   not on the channel's name.
2. **The blind brain reads language with its "visual" cortex** (Bedny et al. 2011, PNAS 108:4429 —
   READ WHOLE): in congenitally blind adults the left occipital cortex responds to sentences over
   harder degraded controls, is sensitive to combinatorial syntax, lexical semantics and phonology,
   and rewires its connectivity toward prefrontal language regions and the thalamus. The authors'
   conclusion, verbatim weight: innate microcircuit properties are NOT necessary for a region to take
   on language. Content follows input and experience.
3. **Sensory substitution works in adults** (Bach-y-Rita's line; Sadato et al. 1996 Nature 380:526 —
   Braille activates primary visual cortex in the blind; Cohen et al. 1997 Nature 389:180 — TMS over
   the occipital pole disrupts Braille reading; cited inside the sources read whole): a channel
   substituted for another still feeds the same knowledge.

**The bound he should accept (the correction, honestly):** Makin and colleagues' «Against cortical
reorganisation» (2022, eLife/PMC — read whole) demolishes the STRONG reading ("any region can take
over any function"): the ferret result stands, but as **input-modality-agnostic COMPUTATION over a
shared architecture**, not pluripotent take-over — A1 and V1 share the same canonical circuit
(feedforward input → recurrent amplification → lateral inhibition), and the behaviour shown was
generic signal detection. Cross-modal effects in blind humans concentrate in higher-order cortex that
is polymodal anyway, and the computations stay domain-specific (the body area responds to bodies
whether by sight, touch or sound — "supra-modal" organisation; the ventral stream's representational
structure is INVARIANT blind vs sighted, Mattioni 2020). The structural blueprint (connectivity +
local architecture) constrains what lands where; critical periods exist (Hubel & Wiesel's ocular
dominance is unmasking of pre-existing inputs, not retuning).

**The machine reading (what survives for us, and it is the strong one):** intelligence = **(i) one
channel-agnostic learning circuit + (ii) routed inputs + (iii) an amodal convergence layer where
knowledge is CONTENT, not channel**. The human brain is not "any organ becomes any other"; it is
"one canonical compute everywhere, inputs routed by a blueprint, contents merged supra-modally".
His claim "no bound to gaining knowledge" is right about the LEARNER (channel-agnostic) and wrong
only about the SUBSTRATE (a brain region carries priors; a channel must EXIST — a human learns
echolocation because ears exist, Kish/Thaler's line). For a machine intelligence the substrate priors
are OURS to choose — that is the machine's advantage, and exactly his point.

## §3 · B — open-ended thought: the generators, named

The human mind's thought stream is NOT one chain. The literature's families (each a candidate
generator for the organism):

1. **The spontaneous-thought family** (Christoff et al. 2016, Nat Rev Neurosci 17:718 — paywalled;
   the framework attested from multiple open secondary sources, flagged): mind-wandering, DREAMING and
   CREATIVE THINKING are ONE family — thought free from deliberate AND automatic constraints. The
   mind's default is not answer-or-null; it is a free generation that the constraints then shape.
2. **Sleep as restructuring, not replay alone** (Wagner et al. 2004, Nature 427:352; Verleger et al.
   2013, Adv Cogn Psych — the authors' own review READ WHOLE at PMC3902672): a night of sleep TRIPLES
   insight on the number-reduction task (above the ~20% base rate); the effect localises to SLOW-WAVE
   sleep restructuring IMPLICITLY acquired regularities into explicit insight. The organism's SLEEP
   currently replays; the human form also RESTRUCTURES (gist extraction, new associations that were
   never heard).
3. **Associative generation** (Mednick 1962's associative hierarchy — cited, not re-read, flagged):
   remote associations are chained through the semantic network; creative people have flatter
   associative gradients (weak links reachable). Alpha's INNER walk over the web IS this form — one
   generator of several.
4. **Analogy / structure-mapping** (Gentner 1983 — abstract-level, flagged): a thought that maps the
   STRUCTURE of one domain onto another — a different generator than association (it preserves
   relations, not word co-occurrence).
5. **Episodic recombination / imagination** (Schacter & Addis 2007's constructive episodic simulation
   — secondary, flagged): future/counterfactual thought is built by recombining memory fragments —
   a mutation operator over episodes.
6. **The inside-out brain** (Buzsáki 2019 "The Brain from Inside Out" — book thesis, flagged; his
   2015 review was read deep in the N-road research): the brain's DEFAULT is self-generated activity;
   perception is a test of its own hypotheses. A mind that only answers when asked is the opposite of
   this: the stream of thought is self-initiated.
