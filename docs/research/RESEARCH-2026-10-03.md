### 2.1 Schmidhuber — «Driven by Compression Progress» (arXiv 0812.4360v2, 2009) [read today]

**What it claims (quoted).**
- Interestingness is the first derivative of compressibility: data «becomes temporarily interesting … once he learns to predict or compress the data in a better way» (abstract); `I(D, O(t)) ∼ ∂B(D, O(t))/∂t` (§2.4, eq. 1).
- The reward: «whenever it learns to reduce the number of bits required to encode the historic data, generate an intrinsic reward … in proportion to the learning progress or compression progress, that is, the number of saved bits» (§1.2, principle 3); `r_int(t+1) = f[C(p(t), h(≤t+1)), C(p(t+1), h(≤t+1))]`, with «both the old and the new compressor … tested on the same data, namely, the history so far» (A.5).
- Error is the wrong quantity: «one should not focus on the errors of the predictor, but on its improvements. Otherwise the system will concentrate its search on those parts of the environment where it can always get high prediction errors due to noise or randomness» (§3.2); «Neither the arbitrary nor the fully predictable is truly novel or surprising» (§2.6).
- The compressor's improvement may run asynchronously and «could be partially performed during "sleep"» (A.6). A self-symbol is a by-product of compressing a history in which the agent is always present (§2.2).

### 2.2 Posani, Wang, Muscinelli, Paninski, Fusi — «Rarely categorical, highly separable representations along the cortical hierarchy» (*Nature*, 2026, open access) [read today]

**What it claims.**
- 14,000+ units from 43 mouse cortical regions (IBL Brainwide Map). Selectivity is categorical at the whole-cortex scale, «within individual regions, categorical representations were rare and limited to primary sensory areas» (abstract).
- Diversity of responses gives high-dimensional geometry and «therefore high separability, allowing linear readouts to separate experimental conditions in many arbitrary ways» (abstract).
- Separability is the fraction of 200 random balanced dichotomies whose cross-validated linear decoding beats the 99th percentile of a label-shuffled null (Methods). On independent conditions it is «near maximal (≥ 0.95) across nearly all cortical areas» (Results, Fig. 6d).
- The number of independent conditions rises along the hierarchy (MIC 5 in SSp-n to 16 in MOs).
- «high separability does not imply a complete absence of structure … representations can possess the generalization properties of low-dimensional disentangled representations and still exhibit maximal separability» (Discussion).
- «in RNNs, simple tasks do not require clusters, whereas complex tasks do» (Discussion, citing Dubreuil et al.).

**Where it bears.**
- Map: the EYES row and the word road. «A cell is a whole look» (#246's reading: on never-seen colour × shape pairs the target had no edge to its own shape word on 210 of 211 items) is the CATEGORICAL extreme: one cell per conjunction. A lookup table separates every condition and generalizes across none.
- The brain's learner is factorised at the word level (a unit per word over the eye's features), so it composes (M3 0.966–1.000), but its 13-word code is low-dimensional.
- Map B10 (growth): what a grown unit should look like.

**Proposal — a SEPARABILITY / CCGP diagnosis (an instrument, rank 8).**
- On the scene world's 60 conditions (6 shapes × 5 colours × 2 sizes), measure three codes: the eye's 146 features, the cells' KWTA activation, and the brain's per-hypothesis word scores.
- (i) Separability by Posani's exact procedure (200 dichotomies, a shuffled-label null at its 99th percentile).
- (ii) Cross-condition generalisation (train a colour decoder on some shapes, test it on the others — Bernardi et al. 2020's CCGP [from memory]).
- **Prediction:** the cells read high separability and low CCGP, the brain lower separability and high CCGP.
- **Plant:** a synthetic code with a planted XOR structure must read non-separable to a linear reader.

**What NOT to take.** The paper is about mice trained on one task. It does not say the brain «should» be non-categorical. It says single regions rarely are, and why diversity pays.
