## What this does NOT show (read before quoting the answer)
- **Frames WITH SLOTS.** Every frame here is an ordered pair of two SPECIFIC words; a slotted construction pays its description once for many instances, which is the point of the SCHEMA design and is NOT tested (the law's §8 item 6 named it as the next law). A negative whole-set G for word pairs says nothing about schemas.
- **That the pairs are «frames» in the human sense**, or that the organism should say them: the cells' mouth is judged by the eye and compression cannot know what the eye sees (the law's §5 item 8).
- **Anything about the shelf text beyond one book collection**, or a different stream: one life's ear rows (five sources), one split (the last 40 % by seq), one set of ten WEB-S seeds.
- **That held-out compression measures USE of a frame in unseen text.** Section POST-HOC: with this add-one unigram code a frame can earn credit it did not earn in the held-out part (the denominator effect), so «G > 0» cannot be read as «the frame shortens unseen utterances» without its anatomy beside it (M, D, C in the report).
- **The post-hoc section is a reading chosen after the unit was read.** Its per-frame credits were computed once, by one script, with its own three controls; nothing in the verdict above depends on it.

## What it means for L1 (SCHEMA) and the compile
- **The criterion discriminates.** «Kept only while they shorten held-out utterances», read as a per-frame test, refuses 84–97 % of what these three selection rules propose and keeps a few dozen frames; it is not a rubber stamp and it is not empty. On SCENE, MDL-GREEDY recovers all 24 of the generator's own pairs.
- **Two defects of the instrument as coded, which L1's organ compressor must not inherit unread:** (i) the whole-set G hides the per-frame verdicts (it is negative for every rule while 39–45 frames pay): L1 judges ONE schema at a time, so the per-frame marginal credit (AMENDMENT-1's (C)) is the quantity to implement, with the whole-set G as a reading; (ii) with an add-one unigram code a frame earns credit through the denominator effect even when it NEVER occurs in the held-out text (6 of each rule's 25 largest credits), so «gain > 0 on held-out» can keep a frame the stream no longer produces. L1's keep rule should credit a frame by its held-out firings (the M term) and report the rest, or hold out the most recent utterances so a stale frame reads zero.
- **The price L = 2 log₂ V a frame is stiff for a pair of specific words** (22.26 bits against MDL-GREEDY's 743 held-out firings saving 3,896.01 bits in all, about 5 bits a firing, derived). Whether a SLOTTED construction clears it is the next law's question (the SCHEMA toy T-L1), and this result is its prior: at pair level, in this split, the paying frames are boilerplate and a few frequent phrases, and the grounded game pairs are credited only by an artefact (the game's text ends before the held-out part starts).
- **What the compile can say honestly:** the held-out gain criterion is a working tool at the per-frame level and finds true structure where there is some; it has NOT been shown to find slotted constructions or to keep frames that matter to speech, and its two defects above are named, measured and fixable.
