### The four rules `believe()` applies, in this order — `genome/think.py:believe`

| order | condition | row written | can it ever REMOVE a cell? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | no standing belief for the word | `belief` | — |

**There is no contraction.** AGM has three operations (§1 (c)); this has expansion (`extension`), a re-affirmation (`revisit`) and a fork (`clash`). **A cell can never leave a standing belief except by the whole belief forking.**

## The three questions only Ahmad can answer

**Q1 — Do you want the creature to reason about what it does not know, when it almost never does not know?**
Measured over nine exams and 2,430 items, the abstentions that a second guess could act on are **12** — half of one percent — and on a thing it has never seen it abstains **180 times out of 180**, perfectly. **Q2 — When he contradicts himself, what should he be willing to GIVE UP?**
His own research asks for AGM — *"minimal change… a dereferenceable address and a traceable provenance chain"*. He has two of the three operations: a belief can **grow** and it can **fork**. It cannot **shrink**, because nothing in the creature says which of two beliefs is worth less. AGM's answer is **entrenchment**: *"the beliefs with the lowest entrenchment should be the ones that are most readily given up."* The creature could rank by the confidence of the recall that made the belief, by how many episodes stand behind it, or by how old it is — **three different creatures.** A belief kept because it is well-evidenced is a scientist; one kept because it is old is a conservative; one kept because it was confidently formed is neither. **Which ordering do you want him to have?**

**Q3 — «Why» — do you want the addresses, or the story?**
`questions.why()` already returns the cells, the episode ids and the edge weights behind any answer, several times an hour, and the door refuses the verb. Wiring it costs nothing.
