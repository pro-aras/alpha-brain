His sentence names a real anatomy. The hippocampal formation does not store a picture; it stores **a thing, at a place, at a time**, and the three arrive on separate wires that converge.

**G-MIND-4 — sleep replays what would CHANGE an answer, not what was most surprising when it happened**

```
SLEEP    every 2000 moments replay 200 by gain need       # parser change: `by surprise` is a literal
```
- **Source:** *"the utility of a backup… the product of a gain and a need term"*, and *"a backup that has no effect on behavior has zero utility even if the target state is expected to be visited in the future"* `[fetched: Mattar & Daw]`; *"only reverse replays increased their rate at increased reward"* `[secondary: Ambrose 2016]`; *"replay… can be modulated by reward or novelty"* `[fetched: Kumaran 2016]`.
- **The two terms, in quantities the creature already stores** — this is the whole reason the line is writable:
  - **gain** = *would replaying this row change an answer I currently get wrong?* Computable exactly, **and without any label, which matters because the organism holds none** (`machinery.py`: *"It holds NO label of any kind"*): run `recall("sight", x_s, k=1)` on the organism **as it is now** and compare its winning WORD CELL id against the word cell the episode's own stored `x_w` resonates. Two cell ids, no truth file. A row whose two ids agree has ~zero gain; a row that disagrees, or abstains, has gain.
  - **need** = *how often will this come round again?* Computable exactly: the count of canvases carrying that word in the last N moments — the maker's world shows things on a cadence, so recency is the honest proxy.
- **The test that could refuse it:** on the SAME replayed tape, after the last sleep before the cut, **Q1 on all 16 words** under gain×need ranking vs under surprise ranking. It must not be **worse**.
- **Ceiling before the bar, and it is the reason the bar is "not worse" rather than a number:** life 2's **no-sleep twin scored .99375 against the creature's .956**, and the reader printed `{"graded": false, "reason": "SLEEP's retention is not gradable on drawn worlds"}` `[from disk]`. **On this world, sleep has no retention to buy back.** Any bar phrased as "gain×need retains N pp more" is a bar above its own ceiling (#228 b). What the line can honestly be judged on is the **selection itself**: the two rankings must differ (Kendall τ printed) and the gain-selected set must have a **lower** pre-replay accuracy than the surprise-selected set — i.e. it really is choosing rows the creature gets wrong.
- **The plant that says the mechanism fires:** insert into the tape a thing shown once early and never again. Surprise ranks it high forever (its encoding error was large and is frozen); gain×need ranks it **down** after the first successful replay and **out** once it stops recurring. If both rankings treat it alike, the need term is not working.
- **Cost:** 200 `recall` calls per sleep = 200 × 0.048 ms ≈ **9.6 ms**, 38 times in 24 h. Negligible, and less than the `most_surprising` it replaces once §2b's repair lands.

## The three questions only Ahmad can answer

**Q1 — Should the moon be a surprise?**
The MODULATION mark failed twice because a memory-relative error cannot spike on a thing that is **new in the world but not novel to the eye** — the moon arrives as a never-heard word on a shape that resonates the circle cell at 0.9–1.9× the steady error. Barto's distinction says these are two different quantities and the creature computes only one. §4 G-MIND-3 offers three arms and they mean different things about what his creature is: **arm A** says *a new NAME is a surprise* (the word is the event); **arm B** says *a thing with no exact memory near it is a surprise even if a prototype explains it* (the episode is the event); **arm C** says *a familiar cell explains more than a fresh one* (the cell's history is the event). **Which of these three is the surprise you want him to have — or is the honest answer that a creature in a world with no consequences should not be surprised by anything except a word it has never heard?**

**Q2 — Should he ever forget?**
The biology is unambiguous and this estate has measured the opposite twice. Davis & Zhong: *"intrinsic forgetting may be the default state of the brain, constantly promoting memory erasure"*, with consolidation as *"the judge, allowing memories deemed important to remain"*; Akers: growing new cells **erases** old memories, and the animals that grow fewest forget least. Here: 0 of 32 usage-ranked pruning arms beat random; E48's two forgetting rules both lost Latin; and life 2 ended with `removed_for_disuse 0` and all-words Q1 **.975**. **A creature that keeps everything is not what any animal is** — and your own note asks about a system that *"recalls too exactly, without abstracting"*. **Do you want a forgetting mechanism in him at all — and if so, is it the FAST ring's age (which he already has) or something that reaches the cells?**

**Q3 — Should remembering change the memory?**
Biology says a retrieved memory is **rewritten** — Nader's reconsolidation is a quarter-century of one of the field's most-replicated results. The creature says the opposite in its own source: *"No birth: questions never change the organism."* That line is not an oversight; it is what makes byte-identical REPLAY possible, and that identity is the strongest instrument this estate has — it caught life 1's wiring defect, it is how every twin in §4 is cheap, and it is how a verdict can be read at all. **Reconsolidation and byte-identical replay cannot both be true of the same creature.** So: **is his exam allowed to change him?** If yes, the estate loses its strongest instrument and gains the mechanism behind belief revision — your own open theory #2. If no, he is a creature whose memories are only ever written by living, never by remembering, and that should be said out loud rather than inherited from a docstring.
